What Matters for World Modeling in Vision-Language-Action Models?
Authors: Qisen Ma, Yixiang Chen, Yuan Xu, Peiyan Li, Jiabing Yang, Kai Wang, Jianhua Yang, Jianlou Si, Jun Huang, Jing Liu, Nianfeng Liu, Yan Huang, Liang Wang
First: 2026-10-14T09:01:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T09:01:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Integrating world models into Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) has emerged as a promising direction, enabling policies to anticipate future dynamics beyond reactive observation-to-action mapping. However, existing approaches vary simultaneously in their world representations, integration paradigms, and policy backbones, making it difficult to determine what truly matters for effective world model integration. To disentangle these factors, we categorize existing efforts into four representative paradigms, namely Implicit Future Alignment, Explicit Future Query, MoT Future Expert, and External World Predictor. We instantiate each within a unified framework and systematically evaluate these paradigms with diverse world representations and policy backbones across in-domain, out-of-domain, long-horizon, dynamic, few-shot, and real-world settings. Our study yields four main findings: 1) world representation effectiveness is paradigm-dependent, whereas 2D semantic representations offer robust, cross-paradigm gains; 2) no single paradigm is universally optimal, yet the decoupled MoT Future Expert offers competitive overall performance, particularly in OOD robustness; 3) world modeling consistently benefits policies built on standard VLMs, while decoupled designs exhibit greater compatibility with pretrained VLAs; and 4) world modeling improves performance over action-only baselines across multiple settings, with notable gains on long-horizon and dynamic tasks. Project page and video demonstrations are available at https://dylanmqs.github.io/WM-in-VLAs/.

Active Exploration in Bayesian Model-based Reinforcement Learning for Robot Manipulation
Authors: Carlos Plou, Ana C. Murillo, Ruben Martinez-Cantin
First: 2024-04-02T11:44:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:48:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Efficiently tackling multiple tasks within complex environments, such as those found in robot manipulation, remains an ongoing challenge in robotics and an opportunity for data-driven solutions such as reinforcement learning (RL). The high cost of data gathering in robotics encourages data-efficient approaches such as model-based RL, which builds a dynamic model of the robot to conduct most policy learning on cheaper simulations, while enabling data reuse and transfer between tasks. In this work, we employ Bayesian deep learning (BDL) with a double purpose. First, we increase the model quality by improving its calibration. Second, we improve data efficiency by performing active learning of the dynamic model during a preliminary exploration phase, actively estimating the information gain of each transition and using it as the exploration reward. We compare several BDL methods in a realistic robot manipulation setup. Our experiments show that our approach matches the quality of relevant alternatives with much lower robot execution requirements and, unlike previous studies validated only on standard RL environments, we tackle robotic arm end-tasks on a complete manipulator simulator such as \texttt{CoppeliaSim}. Code is available at https://github.com/ropertunizar/BayesianActiveExploration.

One Agent per Zone: Decentralised Active Inference under Partial Observability for Multi-Zone HVAC Control on Edge Hardware
Authors: Panagiotis Kasnesis, Lazaros Toumanidis, Christos Chatzigeorgiou, Amalia Contiero Syropoulou, Miguel De Prado
First: 2026-10-14T08:36:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:36:32+00:00
Comments: 9 pages, 3 figures, accepted in European Conference on EDGE AI Technologies and Applications, EEAI 2026, Malta
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-zone HVAC control is a shared-resource problem: zones on a floor share one fan and cooling coil, so each zone's demand affects its neighbours and the building's peak power. One controller per zone scales well and isolates faults, but each controller then observes only its own zone, not the shared air loop. In this work, we present a decentralised controller that assigns each zone an independent active-inference agent, which plans by minimising expected free energy and learns its zone dynamics online. The agents exchange no messages, so none depends on peers or a coordinator; each accounts for the unobserved coupling through a latent disturbance and a congestion signal derived from the metered power of the other floors. Fifteen agents are evaluated in EnergyPlus on a Sinergym office building against three rule-based controllers and three MADDPG fleets, one given the same floor meters. On five noisy weather years unseen during training, the fleet attains the lowest mean building peak (88.3 kW, 3 to 17 % below the baselines) and staggers the floor peaks better than any baseline, with a coincidence factor (building peak over the sum of the floor peaks) of 0.929 versus 0.936 to 0.994. Compared with a reactive rule at similar comfort (0.912 versus 0.916), it lowers the sum of monthly peaks, on which demand charges are billed, by 33 kW (4 %) in every year, at the cost of about 10 % more energy. It costs 9% less per year than the proactive rule (at lower comfort) and 1% less than the MADDPG fleet given the same floor meters, in every test year. The whole fleet runs on a single low-cost Raspberry Pi 5, closing the observation-to-action loop over MQTT in 442 ms at p95, far below the 15-minute control step.

Learning Who Benefits Most: Multi-Treatment Causal Ranking for Population Health Stratification
Authors: Rocco Zaccagnino, Martina Girolamo, Alberto Lombardi, Delfina Malandrino, Donatello Telesca, Alessia Ture
First: 2026-10-14T08:31:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:31:07+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Population health stratification frameworks, such as the Italian DM77, organize patients by clinical need and care pathways but offer limited guidance on which care changes to prioritize under constrained resources. We present PROMETHEUS (Proactive Resource Optimization through Multi-treatment Effect-based Targeting in Healthcare Environments Using Stratification), a framework that formulates population health stratification as a multi-treatment causal ranking problem. Its objective is to order eligible patient-treatment opportunities by expected incremental benefit rather than obtain perfectly calibrated individual treatment-effect estimates. Each clinically admissible care intensification is compared with maintaining the patient's current pathway. Treatments are learned jointly to produce a globally comparable ranking across patients and care profiles, within domains sharing a reference condition, outcome, follow-up horizon, and benefit scale. This separates clinical need, treatment benefit, and resource-constrained allocation. PROMETHEUS combines doubly robust causal signals with a Siamese neural ranking model and contrastive representation pretraining. The shared representation captures similarities across care transitions, supporting prioritization under capacity constraints. Synthetic experiments and semi-synthetic experiments using real administrative covariates from ASL Benevento evaluate ranking quality, allocation value, robustness, and failure modes. The results support the framework's ability to recover generated benefit orderings and prioritize care opportunities in the evaluated settings. PROMETHEUS provides a preclinical approach to actionable stratification; validation with observed treatment-outcome data is required before clinical use.

Refine Actions If Needed: Adaptive Action Chunk Refinement for Robot Manipulation
Authors: Hanlin Liu, Zichen Zhou, Jiacheng Zhang, Liuyuan Na, Jin Jin, Guangyan Chen, Yufeng Yue, Gen Li, Fangqiang Ding
First: 2026-10-14T08:17:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:17:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Computationally lightweight manipulation policies predict executable actions with low inference latency, but prediction errors can limit task success. Iterative generative policies can achieve higher task success rates, but repeated network evaluations increase inference latency. Refining action proposals from computationally lightweight policies can combine these strengths, but the amount of refinement needed varies across states. We introduce RAIN (Refine Actions If Needed), a framework that predicts action errors at different refinement levels to decide whether and how much to refine each executable proposal, before performing any refinement. We train an ensemble to predict these errors by comparing action proposals and their refined versions with expert actions from offline demonstrations, without requiring explicit labels of refinement needs. Calibration balances uncertainty, computation, and temporal consistency. We evaluate RAIN on eight ManiSkill tasks and four real-robot tasks. In simulation, our policy achieves 66.6% success, approaching the best evaluated diffusion-policy success rate with 5.71 times lower policy latency. On the real robot, success increases from 55.0% with Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) to 72.5%, with comparable average successful completion time. Matched controls show that dynamic routing maintains the strongest observed fixed-refinement success level with 26.6% fewer refiner evaluations. Project page: https://rain-policy.github.io/.

Coordi-pi: Coordination-Aware Dual-Hand Action Representation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation
Authors: Yiming Meng, Yanming Shao, Weijie Shen, Kunyang Lin, Jiayu Sun, Ping Luo, Yao Mu
First: 2026-10-14T08:08:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:08:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general-purpose robotic manipulation, but extending them to bimanual dexterous manipulation introduces a high-dimensional and structured action space. Existing bimanual VLA policies commonly concatenate right- and left-side arm--hand actions at each control timestep, leaving cross-hand interactions implicit within a single action vector. We introduce Coordi-$π$, a structured action representation for bimanual dexterous manipulation. Coordi-$π$ represents the right- and left-side arm--hand actions as individual action tokens and organizes them into an interleaved sequence, explicitly exposing the two sides within the action sequence. To explicitly encode hand identity and temporal synchronization in this interleaved representation, a learnable hand-identity embedding distinguishes the two sides, while sequence--control-timestep factorized rotary positional encoding (RoPE) separates sequence position from control timestep, allowing actions at the same control timestep to share a temporal coordinate while retaining distinct sequence positions. Across all five bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in DexJoCo, Coordi-$π$ achieves state-of-the-art performance under both \textit{rand-obj} and \textit{rand-full}, improving the average success rate over $π_{0.5}$ from 31.9\% to 51.3\% and from 18.3\% to 32.3\%, respectively. Coordi-$π$ also consistently outperforms $π_{0.5}$ across four real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks.

One-shot Human Video-Guided Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models
Authors: Qi Chen, Yuwei Wu, Quan Chen, Kunpeng Gao, Jin Chen, Che Sun, Zhi Gao, Yunde Jia
First: 2026-10-14T08:05:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:05:44+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have recently achieved remarkable progress by leveraging large-scale robot datasets and foundation models for general-purpose robot manipulation. However, their ability to rapidly adapt to novel manipulation tasks beyond the training distribution remains limited. Existing approaches often require task-specific robot demonstrations and additional training, which are labor- and compute-intensive, limiting the scalability of VLA adaptation to open-world scenarios. To reduce this dependence on target-task robot data, we propose CapPro, a framework that enables a VLA policy to adapt to previously unseen manipulation tasks using only a single human demonstration video. CapPro consists of two components. First, a phase-aware human video representation module constructs a compact video representation through learned visual token selection and learns task-relevant query representations under phase-caption supervision. Second, a video-conditioned adaptation module jointly incorporates the compact video and query-derived representations into the action-conditioning context of the pretrained VLA policy. % This design introduces task-specific guidance while largely preserving the original VLA action-generation backbone. In this way, the human demonstration provides task-specific execution guidance rather than an embodiment-aligned trajectory to imitate, without requiring target-task robot demonstrations or target-task fine-tuning. Experiments across source and unseen target tasks show that CapPro improves average performance across unseen target groups while maintaining comparable performance on the source tasks. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of human-video guidance for cross-task VLA adaptation.

Abstract
Large protein language models provide a powerful framework for learning sequence-function relationships and guiding experimental design. However, architectural improvements are underexplored, especially in low-compute regimes of relevance for resource-constrained research laboratories. We show that the training cost for encoder-only transformer architectures can be reduced by leveraging modern architectural refinements, offering an encouraging performance / compute-efficiency trade-off. We introduce ProtModernBERT, a modernized BERT-style model pre-trained on amino acid sequences, and compare it with ProtBert, Amplify, and ESM2 across a set of supervised structural tasks -encompassing secondary structure prediction, disorder prediction, and subcellular localization- and ProteinGym's zero-shot functional benchmark. We find that ProtModernBERT matches or closely tracks the performance of Amplify-120M and ProtBert with up to 80-90\% reduction in pre-training FLOPs, and narrows the gap to ESM2 when controlling for pre-training. Our analysis using a cluster-based validation protocol for hyperparameter selection and randomly initialized models disentangles the contributions of architecture and data, showing that modern components provide stronger inductive biases that are more consequential than parameter count in low-compute regimes.

ACRO: Actor-Critic Rollout Orchestration
Authors: Junhoo Lee, Seungyeon Kim, Baekseung Kim, Minkyu Kim, Suhyun Jeon, Jimyeong Kim, Nojun Kwak
First: 2026-10-14T06:55:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T06:55:16+00:00
Comments: 18 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Executing a vision-language-action (VLA) policy involves deciding when to continue an attempt and when to intervene to improve its chances of success. We introduce ACRO, an Actor--Critic rollout orchestration layer that makes these decisions for a fixed VLA. ACRO seeks interventions that offer a more promising continuation, are physically feasible, and leave sufficient time to complete the task. A Critic learned from policy rollouts estimates success prospects to decide when to intervene and select a re-entry target along the executed trajectory. Trajectory Retraction shortcuts the visited path to return the robot to that target, where the same VLA resumes execution from a fresh observation. We evaluate ACRO with GR00T N1.7 and $π_{0.5}$ across SimplerEnv, RoboCasa, and real-robot manipulation. Under the same physical execution budget, including all return motion, ACRO improves success by 10.4, 7.3, and 21.7 percentage points, respectively. It also completes additional tasks as the execution budget grows. These results show that controlling when and where a policy resumes can turn its existing capabilities into more successful task executions. Project page: https://junhoo.me/acro

MobiGuide: Vocabulary-Guided Online Recovery for Mobile Manipulation
Authors: Jia Syuen Lim, Zhizhen Zhang, Peter Bohm, Brendan Tidd, Zi Huang, Yadan Luo
First: 2026-04-02T03:29:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T06:45:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A central challenge in mobile manipulation is preserving multiple plausible action modes while remaining reactive during execution. For example, grasping a bottle in a cluttered scene can be achieved through multiple physically feasible base-arm motion plans. Deterministic VLA policies trained with $L_1$ regression are efficient, but they tend to average across valid modes and may produce physically invalid actions. Diffusion-based generative policies can better represent multimodal action distributions, yet their proposals may be weakly constrained when generated from pure noise, and iterative sampling can increase inference cost. Action chunking helps amortize inference, but it also introduces partially open-loop execution, allowing small mismatches to accumulate into drift. We present MobiGuide, a generative VLA model for efficient mobile manipulation, where action generation is guided by a structured trajectory vocabulary. Constructed from expert demonstrations, this vocabulary encodes plausible whole-body motion patterns and provides structured priors for feasible action generation. Given the current observation and instruction, MobiGuide locally refines these reference trajectories into context-compatible action proposals and selects the most suitable trajectory for execution. This preserves multimodal action candidates while keeping generation constrained around feasible expert-like motion modes. To mitigate chunk-induced drift, we further introduce a lightweight residual correction module that performs high-frequency, per-step adjustments during rollout. Across diverse mobile manipulation tasks, MobiGuide improves success and stability under execution disturbances while maintaining low-latency inference.

ESL-former: Event Sparse Linear Transformer for FPGA-Accelerated Malware Detection
Authors: Jun Yang, Yumeng Cao, Yingxuan Xiao, Changhong Li, Shangru Zhou
First: 2026-10-14T06:42:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T06:42:01+00:00
Comments: Accepted at ICA3PP 2026. 19 pages, 5 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Transformer based intrusion detection has shown strong potential for identifying unknown malware variants, but its deployment on resource constrained edge devices remains difficult due to the high computational and memory cost of self attention. This paper presents an event sparse Linear Transformer framework for malware detection on FPGA edge platforms. Instead of relying on an explicit attention matrix, the proposed method derives token and channel importance directly from the accumulated key value state of Linear Transformer. A contribution aware scoring function is introduced to measure the perturbation of each token or kernel channel to the linear attention state, allowing redundant tokens and low contribution channels to be removed without constructing quadratic attention maps. To translate sparsity into practical hardware acceleration, the proposed design further replaces conventional binary mask based execution with a Top K active token buffer and a hardware compaction unit. The selected active tokens and channels are reorganized into dense fixed length data streams, enabling downstream processing elements to execute regular multiply accumulate operations with reduced redundant memory access and control overhead. This design is suitable for low power FPGA based malware detection, where both latency and on chip resource usage are critical. Experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed sparse Linear Transformer can reduce hardware cost while preserving malware classification accuracy.

Accelerating VLA Inference with Perturbation Informed Execution
Authors: Jehun Lee, Hajun Lee, Jimin Ryu, Youhan Lee, Beomseok Kang, Jonghyun Choi, Jae-Joon Kim
First: 2026-10-14T06:31:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T06:31:06+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 11 figures, 10 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Action-chunking vision-language-action (VLA) models predict sequences of future robot actions. Under fixed-horizon execution, the robot executes a fixedlength prefix of each predicted chunk before invoking the policy again, leaving the remaining predictions unused. For a given number of executed actions, short execution horizons require more policy calls. Extending the execution horizon amortizes policy inference cost over more actions. However, uniformly applying long horizons across episodes can limit opportunities for precise corrections. To address this limitation, we propose PICES (Perturbation-Informed Chunk and Execution Horizon Selection), a training-free method that jointly selects an action chunk and the number of actions executed before the next policy call. Within a single policy call, the action expert generates a batch of action chunks from multiple initial noise samples and their locally perturbed counterparts, using the same conditioning features across all samples. PICES exploits differences between corresponding actions in each nominal chunk and its counterpart to determine candidate-specific execution horizons and select a nominal chunk for execution. Experiments on simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that PICES achieves speedup by up to 2.3x in wall-clock policy inference time on Jetson Orin and Thor relative to fixed horizon baselines while improving the success rate.

Action-chunking vision-language-action (VLA) models predict sequences of future robot actions.

Abstract
Embodied AI agents increasingly require parallel execution of multiple tasks, such as manipulation, conversation, and memory construction, from shared observations under distinct time constraints. Recent Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) support such heterogeneous outputs, yet existing inference systems incur redundant computation and resource contention when serving concurrent tasks on-device. We identify isolated KV cache management as the root cause. To address this, we propose unified KV cache management, an inference design that treats the KV cache as a first-class shared resource across tasks and control cycles. This abstraction enables two key optimizations: cross-task KV sharing eliminates redundant prefill of shared observations, while cross-frame continuous batching batches unfinished language requests from different frames and advances them within a per-frame decoding budget. We also validate the design on Xiaomi-Robotics-0 and StarVLA across different backbones and expert configurations. We further demonstrate the inference gains on a humanoid robot with on-board Jetson AGX Thor.

ReEnvision: Future-Guided Selective Action Repair for Long-Horizon Manipulation
Authors: Xiyin Zeng, Xuewei Huang, Yi Lu, Weihao Yuan, Hao Wang
First: 2026-10-13T08:49:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T06:11:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Long-horizon robotic manipulation requires robust execution over sequences of interdependent actions. Existing methods typically handle execution deviations by replanning the remaining action chunk or correcting actions step by step as new observations arrive. However, a local deviation may affect only a few actions under distribution shift, yet replanning regenerates the entire chunk from an unfamiliar state, potentially introducing errors into otherwise valid actions. Step-wise correction may react too late, as it does not explicitly anticipate how the deviation affects subsequent actions. We propose ReEnvision, a future-guided selective action repair framework that updates a joint future-action belief using execution feedback. It then learns how the resulting belief revision affects each unexecuted action. The resulting belief revision enables selective repair of only the affected actions within a cached action chunk, preserving valid actions and reserving full replanning for severe deviations. On CALVIN, L-CALVIN, and RLBench, ReEnvision achieves 86.6% 5-step success, improves 10-step success from 56% to 63%, and reaches an average success rate of 88.8%, respectively. On five real-world manipulation tasks, it achieves an average success rate of 82.8%. Controlled repair experiments further demonstrate the benefits of localizing affected actions and preserving valid ones during execution.

Abstract
World models have emerged as a promising paradigm for embodied AI, yet existing systems often remain fragmented in the modalities they model and the functions they support. In particular, visual dynamics, geometric structure, and executable action are commonly coupled through separate interfaces or downstream modules rather than being jointly represented and evolved within a shared dynamics model. Extensive experiments demonstrate strong performance across embodied world-model evaluations. Thinker-WM ranks first on EWMBench, WorldOlympiad and WorldArena, while controlled studies further demonstrate the benefits of action conditioning, geometric interaction, and multi-function training within the shared representation.

Abstract

Don't Run with Scissors: Pruning Breaks VLA Models but They Can Be Recovered
Authors: Jason Jabbour, Dong-Ki Kim, Max Smith, Jay Patrikar, Radhika Ghosal, Youhui Wang, Ali Agha, Vijay Janapa Reddi, Shayegan Omidshafiei
First: 2025-10-09T17:07:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T05:23:36+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are a promising paradigm for field robotics, but their deployment is bottlenecked by strict edge SWaP-C constraints. Compression techniques such as quantization, distillation, and pruning are well-established for LLMs and VLMs. Pruning, however, remains particularly underexplored for VLA models. We surprisingly find that pruning VLA models yields drastic performance degradation and increased safety violations. Through spectral analysis, we identify structural properties of VLA weights that help explain this fragility.
