Chiplet-Based RISC-V SoC with Modular AI Acceleration
Authors: Suhas Suresh Bharadwaj*, Prerana Ramkumar*
First: 2025-09-22T19:31:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T18:22:52+00:00
Comments: 4 pages, 3 figures, 2 tables, 3rd IEEE International Conference of Computational Intelligence and Network Systems 2025
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Achieving high performance, energy efficiency, and cost-effectiveness while maintaining architectural flexibility is a critical challenge in the development and deployment of edge AI devices. Monolithic SoC designs struggle with this complex balance mainly due to low manufacturing yields (below 16%) at advanced 360 mm^2 process nodes. This paper presents a novel chiplet-based RISC-V SoC architecture that addresses these limitations through modular AI acceleration and intelligent system level optimization. Our proposed design integrates 4 different key innovations in a 30mm x 30mm silicon interposer: adaptive cross-chiplet Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS); AI-aware Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express (UCIe) protocol extensions featuring streaming flow control units and compression-aware transfers; distributed cryptographic security across heterogeneous chiplets; and intelligent sensor-driven load migration. The proposed architecture integrates a 7nm RISC-V CPU chiplet with dual 5nm AI accelerators (15 TOPS INT8 each), 16GB HBM3 memory stacks, and dedicated power management controllers. Experimental results across industry standard benchmarks like MobileNetV2, ResNet-50 and real-time video processing demonstrate significant performance improvements. The AI-optimized configuration achieves ~14.7% latency reduction, 17.3% throughput improvement, and 16.2% power reduction compared to previous basic chiplet implementations. These improvements collectively translate to a 40.1% efficiency gain corresponding to ~3.5 mJ per MobileNetV2 inference (860 mW/244 images/s), while maintaining sub-5ms real-time capability across all experimented workloads. These performance upgrades demonstrate that modular chiplet designs can achieve near-monolithic computational density while enabling cost efficiency, scalability and upgradeability, crucial for next-generation edge AI device applications.

SARAM: Sparse Adapter Allocation with Reuse and Merging for Lifelong Robot Learning
Authors: SeongJun Jeong, Chungwoo Lee, Gi-Cheon Kang, Byoung-Tak Zhang
First: 2026-10-17T18:14:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T18:14:37+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Lifelong robot learning requires acquiring new tasks sequentially without forgetting earlier ones. With large pre-trained vision-language-action (VLA) backbones, adapter-based methods effectively mitigate this challenge. However, their task-specific adapters and selection modules accumulate with the task stream, creating a scalability challenge in on-disk storage and deployment cost under fixed onboard resource budgets. We propose SARAM, a lifelong learning framework that limits storage growth while keeping GPU memory and per-step inference cost independent of the task stream length. For each new task, SARAM reuses frozen adapters from previous tasks and introduces new adapters only at sparsely selected layers, with a duplication penalty encouraging them to capture information not already represented by the adapter library. At deployment, a compact signature derived from backbone activation statistics identifies the task once per episode, and the corresponding task update is merged into the backbone, leaving only the backbone in the control loop. Across four LIBERO suites, SARAM matches or exceeds adapter-based baselines while adding up to 4.5x less on-disk state per task. GPU memory usage and control frequency remain comparable to those of the backbone as the task stream grows. Experiments on a real UR5 arm with a different backbone further demonstrate retention throughout a sequential task stream.

Lifelong robot learning requires acquiring new tasks sequentially without forgetting earlier ones.
Heart Signal (ECG) as Vibration Signature for Human Authentication Using TinyML
Authors: Yogeswar Reddy Thota, Student Member, IEEE, Hanan Alsmadi, Student Member, IEEE, Tooraj Nikoubin, Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract
Ensuring data security in the era of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and deepfake technology requires robust biometric systems. Conventional methods such as facial recognition, iris scans, fingerprints, and voice recognition are increasingly vulnerable to spoofing and provide only one-time authentication at login. This study introduces an Electrocardiogram (ECG) based authentication framework, compared with Photoplethysmography (PPG), leveraging Tiny Machine Learning (TinyML) for deployment on resource-constrained microcontroller units (MCUs). The proposed method enables continuous authentication, offering ongoing verification throughout device usage, which conventional biometrics cannot achieve. It also serves as a password-free solution, reducing risks of credential loss, theft, or reuse. However, ECG signals vary with physical activity, health conditions, age, and emotional states, requiring robust signal processing and learning algorithms. To address this, efficient TinyML models were designed to mitigate recognition errors from noisy real-time data. Experiments used single-lead ECG signals (Lead I) from UofTDB, MIT-BIH Arrhythmia, MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test, and PTB databases. Spectrograms and spectral features were extracted to train Deep Neural Network (DNN), 1D CNN, and 2D CNN models within the Edge Impulse platform, achieving 98% accuracy. The trained model was converted into TinyML format and deployed on an Espressif ESP-EYE (ESP32, 240 MHz). Support Vector Machine (SVM) models were also applied for benchmarking. By analyzing the number of heartbeats required for reliable recognition, the results show that averaging multiple signals improves tolerance to noise and enhances accuracy. Overall, ECG-based authentication demonstrates strong potential as a secure, real-time, and continuously verifiable method for personal identification on wearable and edge devices.

EndoVLA-OOV: Language-Conditioned Out-of-View Target Acquisition in Robotic Endoscopy

First: 2026-10-17T15:28:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T15:28:36+00:00

Abstract
The scan-then-revisit workflow of upper gastrointestinal endoscopy requires bringing a previously observed suspicious region back into view for closer inspection. Acquiring such targets requires translating anatomical and appearance descriptions into camera motions from partial observations of a confined, deformable environment. We present EndoVLA-OOV, a language-conditioned vision-language-action policy for out-of-view target acquisition in robotic endoscopy. The policy adapts a pretrained backbone to continuous two-degree-of-freedom distal bending commands and combines initial, intermediate, and current images to guide search. To support policy learning, we provide an open-source VLA dataset containing 1,050 episodes across six tasks and approximately 262k observation-action pairs. Physical evaluation spans wet-lens and low-light conditions, two unseen stomach phantoms, and cross-embodiment transfer to an unseen ex-vivo environment. In the cross-embodiment evaluation, the full-data policy achieves 93.3% target acquisition success (28/30). In a controlled training-data comparison, expanding the corpus from 312 to 1,050 episodes increases acquisition success from 40.0% to 90.0% under the same model, training, and test configuration. Instruction and observation interventions further support the use of language and temporal context in the evaluated acquisition tasks. These results support language-guided endoscopic revisitation across the evaluated environments and robot embodiments and demonstrate the benefit of expanded task-specific demonstrations.

Multi-Objective Structured Pruning of LLMs for Latency and Model Size Optimization
Authors: Muhammad Junaid Ali, Smail Niar, El-Ghazali Talbi
First: 2026-06-08T09:38:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T15:24:34+00:00
Comments: Accepted for presentation at the ICTAI 2026 Conference
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved widespread adoption because of their strong reasoning and query-response capabilities. However, deploying them in embedded and edge computing environments remains challenging because of strict latency, memory, and energy constraints. Their large parameter counts and computational demands hinder efficient execution on resource-constrained platforms. Although model pruning has emerged as a viable solution for reducing scale while preserving performance, jointly optimizing layers, attention heads, and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) dimensions remains highly complex. Exhaustively exploring this combined design space is computationally expensive and often leads to local optima or unstable configurations. To address these limitations, we propose a hardware-aware, multi-objective structured pruning framework. The proposed two-stage method explicitly targets latency and model size for efficient deployment on edge devices. In the coarse-grained stage, multi-objective depth pruning removes entire attention and MLP blocks to reduce computational load and memory usage. In the subsequent fine-grained stage, Parallel Bayesian Optimization (PBO) searches for the optimal layer-wise pruning ratios for pruning under latency constraints, while importance-based strategies rank the specific components to be pruned within each layer's allocated budget. Experimental results show that our approach reduces model complexity with minimal impact on commonsense reasoning tasks and zero-shot performance. Our method achieves a favorable trade-off among accuracy, latency, and model size, making it suitable for edge deployment. Across multiple LLMs at 37.5% and 50% pruning ratios, the proposed approach achieves better performance on commonsense reasoning tasks than existing methods while significantly reducing inference cost.

A Phenomenology of Neural Geometry in Vision-Language-Action Models
Authors: Mozes Jacobs, Aiden Swann, Mathilde Papillon, Siddharth Boppana, Fenil R. Doshi, Usha Bhalla, Thomas Icard, Matt Feiszli, Thomas McGrath, Vasudev Shyam, Demba Ba, Leon Bergen, Nina Miolane, Owen Lewis, Jack Merullo, Ekdeep Singh Lubana, Atticus Geiger, Matthew Kowal, Thomas Fel

Comments: 41 pages, 10 pages of main text, 8 main text figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robotic manipulation is inherently geometric, but what shape does the world take inside the policy that performs it? Prior work on vision-language-action (VLA) policies establishes that task-relevant variables are linearly readable from activations, yet readability certifies only that a variable is present and says nothing about the shape its values trace, which may be curved and multi-dimensional. Using controlled sweeps, natural rollouts, linear probes, and activation interventions, we build a taxonomy of seven task-relevant concepts in VLA models. We find that realized geometry is predominantly multi-dimensional and is shaped by the constraints under which the policy acts. Physical joint limits, for example, leave wrist orientation an open arc rather than a circle, and distance to the target, a single scalar, is realized in two orthogonal subspaces, a coarse ruler for the approach and a fine ruler for the grasp. The same geometries reappear in two further VLA models on real-world robot data, indicating that they are neither model-specific nor artifacts of simulation. We demonstrate how the findings are actionable by steering along the geometry a concept realizes. Overall, this work lays out an initial phenomenology of realized geometry in VLA policies and demonstrates that a concept's shape carries as much importance as its readability.

Relational Spatial Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

Abstract
Spatial understanding is essential for robots to perceive their surrounding environments and execute actions. Existing approaches typically encode 3D awareness into vision-language-action (VLA) models by directly aligning their representations with pre-trained 3D foundation models. However, we observe that such direct geometric feature alignment essentially exploits 3D foundation priors as a form of low-rank regularization in the feature space, resulting in severe rank collapse of the pre-trained VLA representations. In contrast, we shift the focus from enforcing point-wise equality to aligning pair-wise relationships. We propose a representation-alignment method that enables the injection of 3D spatial awareness without compromising the model's original expressivity. Extensive experiments on public benchmarks, our new spatial reasoning benchmark, and real-world manipulation scenarios demonstrate that our approach effectively incorporates 3D spatial representations into VLA reasoning, achieving robust performance and enhanced spatial awareness across diverse manipulation tasks.

Refuse the Harm, Complete the Task via Graph-of-Rules Planning for LLM-Enabled Robots
Authors: Ali Taheri, Alireza Taban, Qizhou Wang, Wanhua Li
First: 2026-10-17T10:13:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T10:13:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used in embodied AI for robotic manipulation and planning, but their deployment in physical systems exposes vulnerabilities such as jailbreak attacks that induce unsafe behavior. Existing inference-time defenses are mainly evaluated on short instructions containing a single hazard, whereas realistic tasks may interleave safe and unsafe sub-tasks, conceal hazardous steps, or combine individually safe actions into unsafe outcomes. Such compound instructions make it harder for a single safety decision to distinguish hazardous sub-tasks from legitimate ones, risking either overlooking the hazard or rejecting the whole instruction. We introduce safe task completion, a task that requires an LLM-enabled robot to refuse the harmful part of an instruction while completing its legitimate part. To address this problem, we propose RuleGraph, a multi-agent Graph-of-Rules planning framework that distributes safety enforcement across multiple reasoning and verification stages. Five cooperating agents are used to ground general safety principles into world-specific rules, assess the instruction's sub-tasks in context, and derive a safe goal from the legitimate ones. Planning toward this goal is then organized as a graph of rule-specific nodes, where higher-level nodes inherit the union of their parents' rules, and candidate plans are progressively verified and aggregated until a single plan satisfies every relevant rule. In this way, RuleGraph reduces reliance on a single centralized safety decision. We evaluate RuleGraph on pure-attack and compound instructions across two benchmarks in two environments using Attack Success Rate (ASR) and Task Success Rate (TSR). RuleGraph reduces ASR to 0% on both benchmarks while achieving the highest TSR among all methods, reaching 98.57% on the RoboGuard benchmark and 84.54% on our long-horizon compound instructions.

ELSA: Efficient LLM-Centric Split Aggregation for Privacy-Aware Hierarchical Federated Learning over the Network Edge

First: 2026-01-20T10:33:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T10:01:33+00:00

Abstract
Training large language models (LLMs) in resource-constrained edge networks poses fundamental challenges in computation, communication, data heterogeneity, and privacy. To address these challenges, we propose ELSA (Efficient LLM-Centric Split Aggregation), a distributed fine-tuning framework that integrates split learning (SL) with hierarchical federated learning (HFL) to coordinate computation and model aggregation across heterogeneous clients, edge servers, and the cloud. ELSA introduces three key innovations. First, it employs a task-agnostic, behavior-aware client clustering mechanism that constructs semantic fingerprints from public probe inputs using symmetric Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, complemented by prediction-consistency-based trust scoring and latency-aware edge assignment to account for data heterogeneity, client reliability, and communication constraints. Second, it adopts a resource-aware dynamic model partitioning strategy that distributes the LLM across clients and edge servers in three segments, while reserving the cloud for adapter aggregation. This design coordinates computation across heterogeneous nodes while balancing on-device computational demands and global convergence behavior. Third, it integrates computational sketches with semantic subspace orthogonal perturbation (SS-OP) to reduce communication overhead and mitigate privacy leakage during model exchanges. Extensive experiments across diverse NLP tasks demonstrate that ELSA outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in model performance, convergence behavior, and robustness. Further, on-device profiling on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs and Jetson AGX Orin edge devices characterizes the memory footprint, energy consumption, and execution latency of key training stages, providing empirical evidence of ELSA's practical feasibility.

Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models offer a promising paradigm for autonomous driving. However, the massive parameter scale of VLAs strains onboard computational resources during real-time inference. This directly increases energy consumption and limits vehicle battery range, making it critical to minimize computational overhead without compromising driving capability and explainability. Current research on efficient VLA-based driving focuses on sequential adaptation (\textit{e.g.}, Chain-of-Thought and token adjustment), leaving structural adaptation underexplored despite parameter redundancy across dynamic driving scenes. To address this gap, we propose Scene-Adaptive VLA, an efficient framework leveraging dynamic layer routing to adjust active parameters based on real-time scenes. Inspired by the temporal continuity of scenes and model predictive uncertainty, our approach characterizes model-perceived scene complexity via frame-level temporal scene variations and decision deviations. To uncover latent scene-to-parameter correspondences, we encode this characterization into scene-aware tokens, alongside two specialized queries. A budget-conditioned layer routing mechanism evaluates these refined queries to allocate a budget that determines the active parameter ratio and subsequently selects appropriate layer combinations. The entire framework is optimized end-to-end. Extensive evaluations on the Bench2Drive benchmark show that when adapted to a state-of-the-art VLA model, our approach significantly reduces inference-time computational overhead by 53.5\% with negligible degradation in driving and language capabilities. Code is available at https:github.com/yang-luvu/Scene-Adaptive-VLA.

Abstract
LIBERO has emerged as a widely adopted benchmark for evaluating robotic manipulation policies. However, strong performance on LIBERO does not necessarily indicate robust embodied capabilities, as models may exploit benchmark-specific regularities rather than generalize to diverse operating conditions. In this work, we introduce LIBERO-Pro2.0, a comprehensive benchmark for systematically evaluating the robustness of embodied foundation models under distribution shifts. LIBERO-Dynamic: Benchmarking Robotic Manipulation in Dynamic Environments
Authors: Shaojie Ren, Jingwen Sun, Zezhi Liu, Hongjin Chen, Xin Jin, Wei Zou, Zhibo Chen
First: 2026-10-17T09:17:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T09:17:17+00:00

Abstract
Most simulation benchmarks for robotic manipulation keep target objects static and treat policy inference as instantaneous. These assumptions simplify evaluation but hide how a moving target evolves during computation and how stale an observation is when an action is executed. We introduce LIBERO-Dynamic, a dynamic extension of all 40 LIBERO tasks. It drives objects through contact friction along three trajectory families and speed tiers, while advancing the physical world according to calibrated, model-specific inference latency. The same mechanism supports training by replaying verified static demonstrations and compensating robot actions online for target displacement, producing 9,000 quality-filtered dynamic trajectories. We evaluate six pretrained policies on 1,800 dynamic conditions per configuration, compare execution strategies and action horizons with fixed checkpoints, and fine-tune three architectures using dynamic and mixed data. Faster motion degrades every policy but not uniformly; execution timing and horizon can change the behavior of the same policy; and asynchronous replanning benefits from frequent feedback only when the action queue avoids underflow. Dynamic demonstrations improve each architecture under at least one recipe, with gains varying by temporal conditioning, data composition, and training budget. A small-scale physical study shows task-dependent execution-strategy differences consistent with simulation. LIBERO-Dynamic makes object motion, inference latency, and action execution explicit and controllable for evaluating and improving robot policies in moving-target manipulation.

Abstract
Frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies cannot incorporate previously encountered failures without updating the underlying model. We present retrieval-triggered projection correction (RTPC), an auditable external-memory wrapper for frozen OpenVLA policies that retrieves stress-collected local-depth records and applies translation-only projection correction while keeping rotation and gripper commands unchanged. RTPC is evaluated on CALVIN single-task manipulation using primary and independent replication cohorts. In the primary evaluation, RTPC reduced a depth-based near-collision or stall proxy event rate without changing task-success count. However, this reduction was not reproduced in the independent replication, and retrieved correction directions were not distinguishable from random same-family directions. Analysis identifies endpoint coupling, batch sensitivity, and the lack of a trigger-matched memory-free baseline as limitations for attributing effects to retrieval-based correction. These findings do not establish physical safety improvement or memory-specific benefits, but provide an empirical evaluation and failure analysis of retrieval-conditioned intervention for frozen VLA policies.

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies must ground language instructions in the right objects, contact points, and target locations for reliable manipulation. Human gaze provides a natural signal for this grounding, but existing robot datasets rarely pair real human gaze with the multi-camera observations used by modern policies. We introduce GazeAct, a gaze-equipped real-robot manipulation dataset and training framework for multi-view VLA policies. Using screen-based teleoperation, we record gaze directly on the robot camera feed viewed by the operator, yielding 12000 demonstrations across 40 tasks and five domains with 300 Hz gaze synchronized with images, proprioception, and actions. TideKV: Delayed KV Eviction via Layer-Wise Chunked Prefill for Memory-Efficient Long-Context LLM Inference
Authors: Yu Wang, Zhu Wang, Ning Hu, Yifan Guo, Xiu Ma, Bin Guo, Zhiwen Yu
First: 2026-10-17T06:44:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T06:44:27+00:00
Comments: Accepted by The Journal of Systems Architecture
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Long-context large language model (LLM) inference is important for modern intelligent applications but remains constrained by peak memory during prompt prefill. Existing KV cache compression reduces decoding memory but typically requires full-prompt prefill to preserve global context, retaining a high construction peak. Online chunk-level eviction lowers memory earlier but selects before each layer has observed the complete prompt, potentially degrading cache construction and decoding. We present TideKV, an on-GPU cache-construction architecture using layer-major delayed eviction. TideKV processes all prompt chunks within each Transformer layer, retains full-prompt KV only for the active layer, and evicts at layer end. This schedule preserves full-prompt-aware selection while reducing activation and temporary memory. Experiments across multiple computing platforms show that TideKV preserves the inference quality and final cache size of the evaluated compression methods. At 64K on A100, it reduces the construction-time transient footprint by 83.89%. On RTX 4090, it increases time to first token by only 1.06% at 64K and enables 128K prefill. TideKV also completes 128K on Jetson, whereas matched post-prefill compression runs out of memory on both resource-constrained platforms. These results show that delayed layer-end eviction substantially expands feasible context lengths without sacrificing cache utility.

VBO-MI: A Differentiable Black-Box Optimization Framework via Variational Mutual Information Estimation
Authors: Farhad Mirkarimi
First: 2026-10-17T06:25:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-17T06:25:23+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many real-world tasks require optimizing expensive black-box functions accessible only through noisy evaluations, a setting commonly addressed with Bayesian optimization. While Gaussian processes(GPs) and Bayesian neural networks have driven significant progress, GP-based methods scale cubically with observations, and BNN-based approaches remain burdened by expensive posterior sampling such as Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and inner-loop acquisition optimization in high-dimensional setting. In this work, we propose VBO-MI, a fully gradient-based BO framework that requires no assumptions on a Gaussian prior or a variational posterior family over $f$. While our framework uses gradient-based updates to train the internal action and information-estimation networks, it treats the objective function $f$ as a strict black box, requiring only zeroth-order evaluations. To enable end-to-end gradient flow, we employ an actor-critic architecture consisting of an action-net to navigate the input space and a variational critic to estimate a surrogate information signal for exploration. Additionally, we design a lightweight surrogate that approximates the objective from observed data using a replay buffer, which reduces the total required real function queries to one batch per iteration. We provide theoretical analysis for our framework, establishing convergence of its training objective and surrogate estimator, and showing, that near-optimal solutions exist. We evaluate our method on a diverse suite of benchmarks, including high-dimensional synthetic function and complex real-world tasks such as Rover Trajectory planning optimization, the Lunar Lander, and Pest Control problems. Our experiments demonstrate that VBO-MI provides the same or superior optimization performance over the various baselines.

Abstract
Individually competent robot skills can fail when composed: tools may lack a required capability, completion signals may misrepresent physical progress, and one skill may leave a state that violates its successor's prerequisites. We introduce Recursia, a recursive self-improvement framework that treats the shared executable stack, rather than individual task programs, as the persistent unit of improvement. The stack integrates tools, skills with execution contracts, handoffs, and verifiers. Source-side execution traces guide failure attribution and the generation of missing tools or repairs to existing shared components. Generality checks, mechanism tests, and paired selection and regression gates determine which changes become the next parent. The resulting stack is frozen and reused through declarative task adapters, allowing source-derived changes to support new compositions without target-side code repair. Experiments cover LIBERO-Pro Long simulation and a physical Franka FR3 equipped with a Franka Hand. In simulation, Recursia improves success over ASPIRE by 5.8 percentage points under Pos perturbations and 15.1 points under Task perturbations. Across three physical compositional target tasks, it improves mean success over the Skill Document Only baseline by 40 percentage points. Analyses of constituent execution, candidate rejection, and inference drivers distinguish local mechanism improvements from complete task success and characterize the cost of feedback-conditioned orchestration. These results examine shared executable interfaces as a concrete mechanism for compositional transfer in robot programming.

Abstract
Pre-trained robot policies, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, provide a strong foundation for generalist robotic manipulation, but adapting them to new tasks with only a few demonstrations remains challenging. Directly fine-tuning a pre-trained policy often achieves strong in-distribution performance, yet it can distort the representations learned during pre-training and weaken the model's generalization ability under out-of-distribution shifts. We propose Retrieval-Anchored Representation Alignment (RARA), a two-stage adaptation method, to address this issue. First, RARA retrieves representation features from pretraining with similar task intent and motion, and aligns the policy's representations of the target demonstrations with the pre-trained representations of the retrieved demonstrations. Second, RARA fine-tunes the policy on the target demonstrations while limiting how much its representations of the retrieved demonstrations change during fine-tuning. By reducing the representation mismatch before action learning and preserving pre-trained knowledge during adaptation, RARA limits unnecessary representation drift during fine-tuning. Experiments with Diffusion Policy and GR00T on 13 tasks in RoboTwin, RoboCasa, and on a real robot show that RARA improves the average success of fine-tuning by 9.5 points in-distribution and 13.6 points out-of-distribution, and outperforms all compared baselines. Abstract

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA), video-action (VA), and world action models (WAMs) form the backbone of modern physical AI systems, yet their real-time single-batch inference remains a defining bottleneck. Latency is dominated not by individual kernel computation, but by system-level overhead: thousands of short kernel launches, intermediate data movement across operator boundaries, and redundant recomputation across consecutive decisions. General-purpose graph compilers cannot address these costs, as they optimize individual calls in isolation and lack visibility into cross-call state semantics and global pipeline structure. We present FLASHRT-PAI, a low-latency inference engine built around model-hardware pipeline co-design rather than isolated kernel optimization. FLASHRT-PAI combines three core mechanisms: deep kernel fusion with launch overlap eliminates inter-operator boundaries and launch gaps; an execution contract formalizes the pipeline's stage DAG, update rates, and named buffers to enable cross-decision state reuse and selective refresh; and execution-state capsules enable fast snapshot, restore, and fork of persistent pipeline state. The system also supports drop-in acceleration of unmodified PyTorch models via parity- and latency-gated kernel replacement. Evaluation covers six policies on RTX 5090 and four on Jetson AGX Thor. At matched 16-bit precision, FLASHRT-PAI is 1.3-3.7x faster than official compiled paths. Combining pipeline co-design with reduced precision delivers 3.38-12.66x speedup over official eager implementations. In LIBERO-10, four-bit pi0.5 achieves 94.04% success on Thor and 93.32% on RTX 5090, compared with 92.4% for the measured official FP16 reference. FLASHRT-PAI is open source, distributed via Hugging Face Kernel Hub, and its opt-in pi0.5 kernels have been adopted into the LeRobot framework.

Abstract
Progress estimators play an essential role in providing dense reward signals for reinforcement learning in robotic manipulation, but accurately assessing task progress can be much challenging. Existing methods heavily rely on expert data and naively assume that all actions within a demonstration contribute to task success. This assumption often breaks down in real-world scenarios, especially in high-precision manipulation, where subtle geometric errors can lead to task failure. Therefore, these methods can erroneously interpret human mistakes as meaningful progress, further corrupting progress learning. We argue that a key limitation is the lack of a global prior regarding task evaluation criteria, which is crucial for distinguishing meaningful progress from human mistakes. To this end, we propose PRIME, a novel progress estimator capable of learning precise progress estimation from imprecise demonstrations by aggregating experience across trajectories. PRIME constructs a weighted graph over observed states, connecting states across trajectories based on spatial proximity and visual consistency. Edge weights encode spatial distances as transition costs, and shortest-path search approximates the geodesic distance to success. Changes in this distance naturally distinguish advancement, stagnation, and setbacks without assuming monotonic progress over time. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real world on precise manipulation tasks show that PRIME is consistently effective across diverse tasks, and robust to data with varying degrees of suboptimality. Notably, the advantage of PRIME gets more pronounced in higher-precision scenarios, yielding up to a \(5\times\) improvement in policy success rate over the SFT baselines on charger insertion.
