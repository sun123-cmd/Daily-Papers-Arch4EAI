SafeDriveVLA: Navigation-Conditioned World Model Dreaming for Conflict-Aware End-to-End Autonomous Driving
Authors: Shaoyuan Xie, Zihan Zhang, Jingxuan Wang, Jiashu Qu, Xiaoqing Liang, Lingdong Kong, Junchi Lu, Henrik I. Christensen, Qi Alfred Chen
Venue: CoRL 2026
First: 2026-10-14T17:59:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:59:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted at CoRL 2026. Project page: https://safedrive-vla.github.io/SafeDriveVLA/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are gaining attention in autonomous driving, inheriting commonsense priors from large language models (LLMs) and widening the navigation interface from a fixed command set to free-form natural-language instructions. However, this open-ended interface exposes a new vulnerability surface: an inattentive driver may issue instructions that are unsafe given the current scene, and a malicious user may issue dangerous ones on purpose. We present the first benchmark suite that evaluates navigation following and conflict awareness together, along with adversarial robustness. Existing VLAs fall short on both: SimLingo completes no language-instructed lane change in closed loop, and all models degrade sharply under conflicting signals. We propose SafeDriveVLA, the first driving VLA that (1) explicitly predicts a three-level navigation-compliance mode to decouple conflict reasoning from action generation, and (2) uses navigation-conditioned world model dreaming to inform this prediction. Unlike prior world models that roll out expert or planned actions, ours dreams of strictly following the navigation signal, showing when following is safe. SafeDriveVLA reaches 88.16 Driving Score (DS) and 68.64% Success Rate (SR) on Bench2Drive, lifts closed-loop language following by 49.3% and reduces collisions under unsafe instructions from 60 to 38 compared to the best SimLingo variant. Video demonstrations are available on our project page.

Learning From a Steady Hand: A Weakly Supervised Agent for Robot Assistance under Microscopy
Authors: Huanyu Tian, Martin Huber, Lingyun Zeng, Zhe Han, Wayne Bennett, Giuseppe Silvestri, Gerardo Mendizabal-Ruiz, Tom Vercauteren, Alejandro Chavez-Badiola, Christos Bergeles
First: 2026-01-28T17:03:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:12:58+00:00
Comments: Accepted for publication in IEEE Transactions on Robotics (T-RO), 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper rethinks steady-hand robotic manipulation by using a weakly supervised framework that fuses calibration-aware perception with admittance control. Unlike conventional automation that relies on labor-intensive 2D labeling, our framework leverages reusable warm-up trajectories to extract implicit spatial information, thereby achieving calibration-aware, depth-resolved perception without the need for external fiducials or frame-by-frame manual depth annotation. By explicitly characterizing residuals from observation and calibration models, the system establishes a task-space error budget from recorded warm-ups. Under the evaluated laboratory conditions, the recorded trajectories yielded an empirical lateral error level of approximately 49μm and a conservative axial error estimate of approximately 291 μm during large in-plane moves. In a within-subject user study (N=8), the learned agent reduces overall NASA-TLX workload by 77.1% relative to the simple steady-hand assistance baseline. Together, these results demonstrate successful closed-loop micromanipulation and reduced subjective workload under the evaluated laboratory conditions, without external fiducials or frame-by-frame manual depth annotation, supporting the proposed approach as a framework for microscope-guided robotic assistance.

Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation
Authors: Xiaoman Li, Zhiqiang Yang, Jiayu Hu, Jipeng Kong, Yang Zhang, Jingwei Zhang, Fangzheng Yan, Chunhua Shen, Chenjia Bai
Venue: CoRL 2026
First: 2026-10-14T17:09:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:09:36+00:00
Comments: Accepted at CoRL 2026. 25 pages, 16 figures, including appendix. Project page: https://gr-bc.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1 · Project2
Abstract
Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. New rollouts are incorporated by appending them to the graph and rerunning search, without training a reward model, action-conditioned critic, or progress predictor. Across four precise manipulation tasks and three VLA backbones, GR-BC improves single-round supervised fine-tuning and reaches 91% success after a few offline iterations, without trajectory comparisons or progress labels. What the Elevation Map Cannot See: Semantic-Aware Locomotion and Execution-Aware Navigation for Humanoid Robot
Authors: Shunyu Yao, Songyang Liu, Dinghao Chen, Yuanyuan Lei, Shuai Li
First: 2026-10-05T21:08:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:03:04+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Navigation for humanoid robots is critical, yet large-scale evaluation on physical hardware is often impractical due to cost and safety concerns, making simulation benchmarks essential. Existing VLN benchmarks achieve physically executable navigation, but still assume (1) all hazards are observable from elevation maps; (2) realized motions closely match desired motions. In real environments, however, fallen bottles may be ambiguous in elevation maps, while phones and water spills may be difficult to differentiate; hazard avoidance by the locomotion policy can cause the robot's actual trajectory to deviate from the path intended by the VLN policy. Such command-execution mismatch can accumulate and lead the robot toward unintended locations. To expose these failure modes, we introduce a benchmark that models both elevation-subtle hazards and execution deviations, together with a closed-loop VLN + locomotion control framework that continuously realigns high-level navigation with the robot's actual state. We evaluate navigation in simulation and further validate the locomotion policy on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Results show that semantic input reduces contact with hazards poorly represented in elevation maps, while anti-deviation improves navigation success. These findings highlight the need to evaluate humanoid navigation jointly in terms of route completion and hazard avoidance.

Abstract
It has been difficult to accurately model the physics of the interaction between a robot and the objects it manipulates, including contact mechanics, coupled contact dynamics, deformations, stiffness and friction. We present RoboSimStudio (RSS), an interactive toolkit that makes choices of solver, contact model, and physical parameters explicit and editable, allowing both humans and coding agents to quickly create and iteratively refine simulations based on observed physical behavior. We implement RSS with the open Newton physics simulation system and describe experiments with human-guided real2sim using (1) visual evaluation, (2) force-feedback and (3) agentic Real2Sim2Real. AdaRSI: On-Demand Recursive Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents
Authors: Peidong Wang, Ying Chang, Zhiming Ma, Xiaocui Yang, Shi Feng, Daqi Liu, Yuzhe Yang, Xu Cao, Chuang Wang, Jian Su, Daling Wang
First: 2026-10-14T16:51:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:51:09+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) often fail midway through unseen long-horizon tasks. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) offers a remedy, but embodied RSI methods either refine the harness around a frozen executor and so cannot add a missing motion, or train the executor on persistent failures without asking whether a failure lies in how the executor is called or in what it can do. We ask when a self-improving embodied system should change the weights of its executor, and propose AdaRSI, an on-demand RSI framework for embodied agents. A coding agent first improves its skills and harness, then attributes persistent failures by retrying failing sub-steps from saved states, and fine-tunes only the sub-steps that remain unreliable, with demonstrations it collects itself and experience carried over from earlier tasks. On all 50 target tasks of RoboCasa365, with GR00T N1.5 as the executor and no human demonstrations, AdaRSI improves the VLA on all three suites and raises its success on unseen composite tasks from 4.6% to 13.8%. Compared with fine-tuning every sub-step, it reaches slightly higher success while using only 28% of the GPU time and 22% of the self-collected demonstrations, and fine-tuning without a harness trains more yet gains far less.

Interaction Latents as Actions for Contact-Rich Manipulation
Authors: Yuhang Zheng, Yujie Zang, Yuchen Liu, Yupeng Zheng, Zhennan Jiang, Zebin Xing, Shuai Tian, Songen Gu, Jingxiang Guo, Chen Gao, Zhongpu Xia, Shuicheng Yan
First: 2026-10-13T17:59:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:29:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Contact-rich manipulation requires robots to coordinate not only where to move, but also how contact and force should evolve throughout an interaction. Yet most robot policies represent actions primarily as motion trajectories, using tactile signals as inputs or auxiliary prediction targets without explicitly encoding contact dynamics in the action representation. We propose ILA (Interaction Latents as Actions), which uses decodable interaction latents that jointly capture the evolution of motion, contact, and force application as policy action units, providing a shared representation for action generation, evaluation, and offline improvement. We learn these latents through visually conditioned joint action-tactile reconstruction with a time-conditioned implicit decoder. Using them as flow-matching generation targets supports post-training of pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world action models (WAMs), as well as training flow-matching policies from scratch. A shared decoder maps generated latents to executable actions. For offline improvement, we learn a tactile-conditioned expert reference model from interaction latents encoded from expert demonstrations. The resulting expert-consistency rewards guide weighted learning from successful rollouts, while observation-compatible expert references guide correction of low-reward predictions from failed rollouts. Across three policy families, ILA improves success rates averaged over four real-world tasks by 21.0-36.5 percentage points relative to tactile-conditioned direct-action baselines. Ablations highlight the benefit of encoding contact evolution in action targets beyond tactile conditioning, while the same latent space supports expert-guided offline improvement. X-Safe: Embodiment-Agnostic Safeguarding for Robot Arm Manipulation Policies via Action Masking

Abstract
Ensuring safety of learning-enabled robotic manipulation across diverse embodiments and tasks still requires significant manual engineering. For collision avoidance, existing approaches typically rely on embodiment-specific learned safety approximations or simplified robot models together with stopping-based fallback controllers that compromise performance. In this paper, we propose an embodiment-agnostic safeguarding approach, X-Safe, which provides formal probabilistic guarantees for collision avoidance without model simplifications or learned safety functions. By operating in the configuration space, our safeguarding method works with different types of robot policies. X-Safe reasons locally around the current configuration to establish safe regions and uses action masking to restrict the policy's support to these safe regions. We demonstrate X-Safe on two embodiments and three policies, both in simulation and on hardware. We observe less than half of the performance degradation compared to state-of-the-art safeguarding, and empirically corroborate our formal collision avoidance guarantees.

When Do Discrete Actions Make Good Continuous Controllers?
Authors: Zeji Yi, Soham Dasgupta, Xinyang Zhang, Koushil Sreenath, Guannan Qu, Xi Chen, Max Simchowitz, Guanya Shi
First: 2026-10-14T16:20:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:20:58+00:00
Comments: 53 pages (12 main text), 19 figures, 12 tables. Project page: https://iscoyizj.github.io/discrete-action-website/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1 · Project2
Abstract
Though modern AI models like LLMs predict discrete tokens, today's robotic foundation models represent actions as continuous vectors. In this paper, we challenge this common consensus by asking if continuous action parametrizations are superior to their discrete counterparts. We conduct an extensive comparison of single-step regression and multi-step flow variants of both continuous and discrete policies in both from-scratch single-task and multi-task VLA training, in both simulation and on real hardware. In all regimes, we find that discrete action prediction, even the most naive, uniform-bin quantization, can match or even outperform continuous-action policies in task success rate. Moreover, we find that the challenge to discrete action policies is \emph{not} action precision, but rather factorization error introduced by product-distribution predictions across time and action dimensions. We show that, when paired with the correct interventions---iterative computation through discrete flows, or distribution sharpening through classifier-free guidance---discrete flow policies can turn this weakness into a strength, enabling more responsive action changes during a predicted action chunk. Lastly, we show that attempts to explicitly inject geometric information into discrete policies, e.g. through corruption processes that emulate continuous flow matching, do \emph{not} yield consistent improvements. Taken together, our findings question existing assumptions that robotic action prediction requires distinct design decisions compared to other AI domains, and motivates the possibility of discrete action prediction as a compelling alternative to today's continuous action parametrizations. Hyperspherical Contrastive Representations Unlock Planning and Exploration
Authors: Jonghae Park, Sungil Son, Jonghun Shin, Junseo Lee, H. Jin Kim, Benjamin Eysenbach
First: 2026-10-14T16:08:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:08:28+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://jonghaepark.github.io/c-slerp
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
We develop a self-supervised reinforcement learning algorithm that learns to build towers of up to eight cubes from scratch, without prior data, rewards, demonstrations, or pretrained models. Our method, Contrastive Planning via Spherical Linear Interpolation (C-Slerp), uses contrastive representations as reusable building blocks and repeatedly composes them to infer a sequence of intermediate waypoints. Under our hyperspherical parameterization, the learned representations allow intermediate waypoints to be inferred in closed form through spherical linear interpolation, without training a separate high-level policy or performing graph search. Importantly, the representation geometry also tells us how to adjust the number of waypoints based on the difficulty of each goal-reaching problem. Experiments show that our method succeeds on long-horizon navigation and robotic manipulation tasks, including settings where strong baselines fail to achieve a single success. Ablations further show that using a fixed number of waypoints substantially degrades performance, highlighting the importance of adapting the plan to task difficulty.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for robotic manipulation, enabling robots to generate actions from visual observations and language instructions. However, most existing VLA models still operate on image-level visual representations inherited from pretrained vision-language backbones, while lacking fine-grained object understanding for manipulation. As a result, their robustness can degrade under out-of-distribution object layouts, clutter, and background variations. To address these limitations, we introduce ObjDream, an object-centric VLA framework centered on object-level understanding and future object state prediction. ObjDream uses a spatio-temporal object encoder to organize visual observations into temporal object slots, where persistent object identity and dynamic object state are represented through separate channels. These structured object representations are inserted into the token sequence of the vision-language model (VLM), where identity-guided routing modulates VLM attention to the corresponding object tokens. In addition, an action-conditioned object dynamics module predicts compact future object states relevant to manipulation during training, explicitly supervising object state evolution under robot actions without requiring future prediction at inference. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin 2.0 show that ObjDream improves generalization under diverse distribution shifts and scene perturbations. Real-world robot experiments further demonstrate that ObjDream transfers to physical manipulation settings with novel object arrangements and background changes.

PoLAR: Factorizing Extent and Mode in Latent Actions for Robot Policy Learning
Authors: Youngjoon Jeong, Jihwan Yu, Minsoo Jo, Junha Chun, Taesup Kim
Venue: CoRL 2026
First: 2026-06-19T06:24:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T12:18:57+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://joon-stack.github.io/PoLAR, Accepted to CoRL 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robot actions vary in both extent and mode: how much change they produce and what kind of change they produce. A latent action space that reflects this structure may make it easier for downstream policies to map latent actions to robot actions. However, existing latent action methods typically rely on visual reconstruction from observation pairs without explicitly encouraging such structure. We introduce Polar Latent Actions with Radial structure (PoLAR), which imposes a radial-direction structure on latent actions, encouraging radius to encode transition extent and direction to retain transition mode. PoLAR uses temporal offsets between observations as a weak proxy for transition extent, encouraging transitions with larger offsets to occupy larger radii. We instantiate this structure in hyperbolic space, whose expanding volume with radius offers a natural fit for more diverse transition modes at larger extents. Across in-task and large-scale pretraining settings, PoLAR improves downstream policy performance in simulation and real-world robot experiments, outperforming latent action baselines and strong pretrained VLAs. These results suggest that structuring latent actions by extent and mode helps transfer visual pretraining to robot policy learning.

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have rapidly advanced generalist robot manipulation by harnessing the semantic knowledge of pretrained vision-language backbones. However, their visual representations are typically grounded in 2D image coordinates rather than calibrated camera geometry. This limitation is especially pronounced in multi-camera systems, where views are coupled by known intrinsics and extrinsics but processed as independent images. We propose G$^3$VLA, a camera-aware geometric module that injects calibrated structure into the visual-token stream of a pretrained VLA without altering its action space or original action-learning objective. Controlled calibration studies across $π_0$ and $π_{0.5}$ show that these gains depend on correct camera geometry while remaining stable under mild calibration perturbations. These results demonstrate that grounding VLA visual tokens in calibrated camera geometry provides a simple and general mechanism for improving geometric consistency, multi-view reasoning, and viewpoint robustness in pretrained robot policies.

FoveaVLA: Foveation Actions Replace the Wrist Camera in VLAs
Authors: Bingao Chen, Lihan Zha, C. Karen Liu, Guy Tevet
First: 2026-10-14T12:04:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T12:04:21+00:00
Comments: Project website: https://tml.stanford.edu/FoveaVLA/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present FoveaVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that replaces the wrist camera with learned foveation actions. Wrist cameras provide close-up detail but restrict the view to the end-effector's surroundings. External cameras see the whole scene, but the policy receives them at low resolution, so the fine detail needed for manipulation is lost. FoveaVLA recovers this detail by predicting, alongside each action chunk, the interaction region to observe at the next policy query. The policy then receives crops of these regions, taken from the original-resolution images, together with the full views. We build FoveaVLA on $π_{0.5}$ in two configurations, Single and Dual, using one or two external stereo cameras with full and foveal RGB and depth inputs, and post-train both on DROID with RoboInter interaction annotations. Across four real-world task suites, FoveaVLA-Single achieves 70.8% success with one external RGB-D view, and FoveaVLA-Dual achieves 77.1%, versus 52.1% for the wrist-equipped $π_{0.5}$. Both match the baseline on plain pick-and-place and improve success with distractors, cloth manipulation, and far-away objects.

Abstract
Progress toward general intelligence in the physical world requires scaling robot learning across diverse environments and interactions. However, existing robot policies use shared parameters that remain fixed across interaction contexts. When different operations favor competing parameter adjustments, fitting their mixture can leave individual operations insufficiently adapted. Our atomic-task diagnostics reveal competing update directions in both vision-language and action parameters, with source-improving updates increasing loss on other tasks. This motivates MorphAct, a contextual parameterization framework that dynamically adjusts robot policy parameters as interaction contexts change. Two coupled generators produce context-dependent low-rank updates for the vision-language and action modules. Both generators are trained jointly through the native action objective, without operation labels or expert-adapter targets. MorphAct improves performance across three VLA backbones on LIBERO and Meta-World, with gains of up to 16.30%. In real-world evaluation, it achieves gains of up to 14% over $π_{0.5}$. SnapFlow: One-Step Action Generation for Flow-Matching VLAs via Progressive Self-Distillation
Authors: Wuyang Luan, Junhui Li, Weiguang Zhao, Rui Ma, Tieru Wu

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models with flow-matching action heads, such as π0 and π0.5, have become a standard recipe for generalist robot manipulation, but every action chunk is produced by integrating a learned velocity field over about ten sequential action-expert evaluations, so action generation dominates model latency and caps the achievable control frequency. Existing remedies either shrink each evaluation through layer distillation or pruning while leaving the sequential solver in place, or import one-step objectives from image generation that were designed for training from scratch, rely on an exponential-moving-average teacher or an external critic, and regress finite-span targets onto the conditional velocity, whose variance around the marginal field biases the learned map. We introduce SnapFlow, a self-distillation method that adapts a pretrained flow-matching VLA to one-step action generation. SnapFlow mixes local flow-matching supervision with shortcut samples whose target is the model's own two-step Euler endpoint under stop-gradient, and adds a single zero-initialized destination-time projection so that one action expert serves both objectives while the pretrained predictor is preserved at initialization; no separate teacher network or critic is required. On π0.5, whose 10-NFE teacher operates near ceiling, the archived one-NFE SnapFlow checkpoint reaches 98.75% LIBERO success versus 97.75% for the teacher; under a separate paired evaluation of π0, the one-NFE model surpasses its teacher by 10.5 percentage points. SnapFlow also lowers mean and tail action error on held-out data, cuts batch-one model latency by about 3.3x, and transfers to shelf retrieval on a dual-arm humanoid.

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract
Multi-zone HVAC control is a shared-resource problem: zones on a floor share one fan and cooling coil, so each zone's demand affects its neighbours and the building's peak power. One controller per zone scales well and isolates faults, but each controller then observes only its own zone, not the shared air loop. In this work, we present a decentralised controller that assigns each zone an independent active-inference agent, which plans by minimising expected free energy and learns its zone dynamics online. The agents exchange no messages, so none depends on peers or a coordinator; each accounts for the unobserved coupling through a latent disturbance and a congestion signal derived from the metered power of the other floors. Fifteen agents are evaluated in EnergyPlus on a Sinergym office building against three rule-based controllers and three MADDPG fleets, one given the same floor meters. On five noisy weather years unseen during training, the fleet attains the lowest mean building peak (88.3 kW, 3 to 17 % below the baselines) and staggers the floor peaks better than any baseline, with a coincidence factor (building peak over the sum of the floor peaks) of 0.929 versus 0.936 to 0.994. Compared with a reactive rule at similar comfort (0.912 versus 0.916), it lowers the sum of monthly peaks, on which demand charges are billed, by 33 kW (4 %) in every year, at the cost of about 10 % more energy. It costs 9% less per year than the proactive rule (at lower comfort) and 1% less than the MADDPG fleet given the same floor meters, in every test year. The whole fleet runs on a single low-cost Raspberry Pi 5, closing the observation-to-action loop over MQTT in 442 ms at p95, far below the 15-minute control step.

Abstract
A central challenge in mobile manipulation is preserving multiple plausible action modes while remaining reactive during execution. For example, grasping a bottle in a cluttered scene can be achieved through multiple physically feasible base-arm motion plans. Deterministic VLA policies trained with $L_1$ regression are efficient, but they tend to average across valid modes and may produce physically invalid actions.
