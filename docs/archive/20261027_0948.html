BriLLM-MetaPred: A Representation-Prediction Unity Language Model with Adaptive Self-Measurement
Authors: Junyuan Liu, Hongyue Lin, Jiale Hong, Yi Zhao, Hai Zhao
First: 2026-10-25T03:08:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T03:08:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present BriLLM-MetaPred, the first engineering implementation of the MetaPred machine-learning framework established by a mathematical theory of AGI, instantiated for language modeling. The theory argues that MetaPred is both necessary and sufficient as a machine-learning primitive for general cognition. Taking this theoretical conclusion as a design premise, we report an implementation of the primitive for language modeling. A single continuously evolving dynamical state serves as both the context representation and the state from which predictions are read out. Continuous measurement of its own state reduces finite-depth inconsistency, maintains internal consistency, and provides an endogenous dynamical self-supervision signal in fixed-point form. The single dynamical state of MetaPred intrinsically couples training and inference. MetaPred therefore exhibits symmetry across almost all key elements of machine learning, an intuitive and observable property of the mathematical optimum over the space of all machine-learning paradigms. BriLLM-MetaPred is neither another recurrent language model nor an extension of BriLLM. It provides a bridge between two prior contributions: the construction and motivation of the MetaPred primitive provided by AGI theory, and the SiFu learning paradigm provided by BriLLM (Zhao et al., 2025). The latter's node-signal-flow design enables the primitive to run on available software and hardware accelerators. Our implementation of BriLLM-MetaPred achieves predictive performance comparable to a GPT baseline of the same size. This provides initial evidence for the engineering feasibility of the MetaPred framework established by the mathematical theory of AGI. MetaPred is the first machine-learning paradigm derived entirely from mathematical theory rather than assembled and incrementally improved through human design---and perhaps the last such paradigm.

SentryVLA: Uncertainty-Guided Action Selection and State Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Robotic Control
Authors: Zongyao Lyu, Lingling Chen, William J. Beksi
Venue: CoRL
First: 2026-04-17T20:20:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T02:55:19+00:00
Comments: To be published in the 2026 Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as generalist robotic controllers that map visual observations and language instructions to continuous actions. However, pretrained VLAs typically provide limited information about the reliability of their generated actions, making deployment challenging when execution failures must be anticipated. We address this problem by introducing SentryVLA, a lightweight runtime wrapper for frozen VLA policies. At the action level, SentryVLA uses conformalized quantile regression to estimate a calibrated upper bound on the deviation between sampled VLA actions and expert-like behavior under the calibration distribution. The predicted deviation is then used to rank multiple stochastic candidates for action selection. At the execution-monitoring level, SentryVLA applies a Mahalanobis-distance-based anomaly score to the contextualized latent execution state extracted before final action prediction, providing a complementary signal for detecting abnormal execution states during closed-loop control. The framework does not require retraining or architectural modification of the underlying VLA policy. Experiments in simulation and on a physical robot show that SentryVLA improves action selection and provides useful runtime warning signals for abnormal execution states. These findings demonstrate the practical value of post-hoc reliability scoring for improving the runtime behavior of pretrained VLA policies. LaWAM: Latent World Action Models for Efficient Dynamics-Aware Robot Policies
Authors: Jialei Chen, Kai Wang, Kang Chen, Shuaihang Chen, Feng Gao, Wenhao Tang, Zhiyuan Li, Weilin Liu, Zhuyu Yao, Boxun Li, Yuanbo Xu, Chao Yu
Venue: CoRL 2026

Comments: Accepted at CoRL 2026. Project website: https://rlinf.github.io/LaWAM/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining for semantic robot control, but often lack explicit foresight into how robot actions change the scene. World-Action Models (WAMs) address this limitation by conditioning policies on predicted futures, yet existing approaches typically rely on computationally expensive video generation with substantial pixel-level redundancy. We present LaWAM, a Latent World Action Model that exposes predictive dynamics to robot policies through compact latent visual subgoals instead of reconstructed future video. At the core of LaWAM is a latent-action-conditioned Latent World Model (LaWM). We obtain LaWM by training a latent action model in the latent space of a pretrained vision foundation model and repurposing its forward decoder to predict future observation features for scene evolution. LaWAM then conditions action generation on these predicted latent visual subgoals to enable dynamics-aware robot control. LaWAM achieves state-of-the-art or competitive success rates (SRs) across LIBERO (98.6% SR), RoboTwin (91.22% SR), and real-world manipulation tasks while retaining low-latency inference. LaWAM runs in 187 ms per action-chunk prediction and achieves up to 24x lower wall-clock latency than pixel-space WAMs.

Abstract
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) is a fundamental task in affective computing that enables applications such as mental health assessment, human-computer interaction, and automated customer support systems. Accurate emotion classification from speech remains challenging due to variations in speaker style, acoustic conditions, and the high computational cost of modern deep learning approaches. Many existing systems depend on large transformer-based architectures that require substantial computational resources, making them less suitable for real-time or resource-constrained environments. This research proposes EmoNet, a lightweight deep learning framework for speech emotion classification across seven emotion categories. The model is built on a four-block depthwise separable CNN encoder with squeeze-and-excitation attention and residual connections, combined with a Bidirectional LSTM for temporal feature learning and a multi-head attention pooling mechanism for improved representation. A comprehensive 134-channel acoustic feature set alongside data augmentation techniques including audio-level perturbations and SpecAugment is employed to improve robustness under diverse acoustic conditions. Evaluated on a combined dataset of TESS, RAVDESS, and SAVEE, EmoNet achieves a test accuracy of 96.75% with only 4.11 million parameters. The proposed model outperforms its reproduced baseline and remains competitive with significantly larger state-of-the-art architectures.

Abstract
Learning robot manipulation policies from human video demonstrations constitutes a promising avenue for scalable robot learning. However, comparing different human-to-robot (H2R) transfer methods remains challenging, as existing approaches are evaluated under different settings, including differing task suites, scene layouts, object instances, and amounts of robot supervision. To address this challenge, we present H2RBench, a Real2Sim benchmark for evaluating H2R transfer methods. H2RBench provides a standardized protocol built on real human video demonstrations and simulated robot demonstrations, and includes four manipulation tasks spanning diverse interaction requirements. We evaluate multiple representative H2R transfer methods, each adopting a different strategy for bridging the embodiment gap. Using H2RBench, we systematically characterize how each method scales with the amount of human demonstrations, revealing that methods differ substantially in their ability to leverage additional human data. We further show that simulation performance is broadly predictive of real-world robot performance, with an overall Pearson correlation of r = 0.89, Spearman correlation of \r{ho} = 0.85 and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) of 0.06 across method-task configurations. These results establish H2RBench as a practical and scalable benchmark for comparative H2R evaluation prior to real-world deployment.

Abstract
Running vision-language navigation fully onboard an aerial robot is hard, since grounding, planning, and control must share limited compute and a single-stage error is difficult to isolate in flight. End-to-end aerial policies fuse these stages into one network, giving up the observability and safety checks a modular stack keeps available. We propose VLN on the Fly, an onboard stack that keeps grounding, planning, and control as separate, inspectable stages. A quantized VLM grounds an instruction to a coarse image cell, depth lifts it to a 3D goal, a fast B-spline planner returns a feasible trajectory, and a pretrained reinforcement learning policy tracks it to motor commands across quadrotors. Across 15 onboard flights over three everyday referents in a controlled indoor volume, the stack reaches the target in 13 of 15 trials with 5.72 cm mean goal error and 39.3% average GPU utilization. In 6 additional cluttered-environment trials, the stack tracks collision-free trajectories under onboard perception gating.

DiVeR: Decision-Critical Verifier Learning for VLA Test-Time Scaling
Authors: Seongheon Park, Heecheol Kim, Shulin Tian, Lilika Makabe, Namiko Saito, Katsushi Ikeuchi, Sharon Li, Yasuyuki Matsushita
First: 2026-10-04T04:25:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T00:46:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Scaling robot data and model capacity has improved Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, but further progress is constrained by the high cost of robotic data. Verifier-guided test-time scaling offers an efficient alternative by sampling multiple action candidates and selecting the one most likely to lead to task success at inference time. Existing classification-based verifiers learn from trajectory-level outcomes but treat all visited states equally, even though their value for candidate discrimination can vary across a trajectory. At many states, plausible actions are similar and provide limited discrimination signal, while only a sparse set of decision-critical states admits meaningfully different actions that can substantially affect downstream outcomes. To address this, we propose DiVeR, which estimates decision criticality from the dispersion of sampled action representations. DiVeR then uses this signal to reweight verifier learning toward states where action selection is most consequential, without requiring step-level annotations or additional environment interaction. Across LIBERO, RoboCasa, and real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 robot, DiVeR consistently improves task success through more effective verifier-guided action selection, while adding negligible verifier inference overhead.

Securing Edge & Physical AI: Post-Quantum IoT Blockchain for Mission-Critical Cyber-Physical Agents
Authors: Kürşat Çakal, Suat Özdemir
First: 2026-10-24T19:06:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-24T19:06:19+00:00
Comments: 47 pages, 39 figures, 10 tables, 5 algorithms, to be published in IEEE IoTJ, Science Directy (Elseiver IoTJ, Embodied Intelligence in Physical Systems)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Distributed Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) and Edge AI networks increasingly depend on public-key authentication and key-establishment mechanisms that are vulnerable to cryptographically relevant Quantum Computers (QC). Shor's algorithm threatens widely deployed asymmetric primitives, while Grover's algorithm reduces the effective security margins of symmetric and hash-based mechanisms. Although Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) provides a migration path, its larger public keys, ciphertexts, and signatures introduce additional computation, memory, communication, storage, and packet-fragmentation costs that can constrain dynamic multi-agent systems. In this article, we introduce rotational secrecy, an architectural objective that limits temporal key exposure through authenticated transitions between cryptographic epochs. We present Private Authentication for Rotational Secrecy (PARS), a reference mechanism that coordinates policy-triggered key establishment, rotation, and reconciliation among authorized Physical AI agents. For deployments that need distributed agreement on security state, we further develop the PARS-Enabled Edge Security Ledger (PARS-ESL), an optional permissioned architecture maintained by authorized validators or capable edge gateways. PARS-ESL does not store high-volume operational transactions. Instead, it records authenticated state for key epochs, group membership, revocation, algorithm-suite policies, challenge validation, and recovery checkpoints. As a result, resource-constrained agents can take part as lightweight clients, and low-latency operational traffic stays outside the ledger consensus path, protected by the currently active symmetric epoch keys. Our results show that ledger-assisted post-quantum key-epoch management is feasible for selected Edge AI and CPS security-control workloads under the stated trust, compromise, connectivity, and resource assumptions.

Abstract
Achieving general-purpose robotic manipulation requires robots to bridge high-level semantic intent with low-level physical interaction in unstructured environments. However, zero-shot generalization remains challenging: end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack the precision required for long-horizon tasks, while traditional hierarchical planners suffer from semantic rigidity under open-world variations. First: 2026-10-24T17:42:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-24T17:42:49+00:00

Abstract
The pervasive deployment of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) across the computing spectrum has brought growing attention to energy efficiency, alongside raw performance. While voltage scaling is a potent knob for power reduction, its application in next-generation nodes is hampered by severe throughput degradation and susceptibility to process variation. This work aims to address these energy-reliability trade-offs by introducing a hardware-software co-design methodology, based on the observation that timing-robust bit ranges within the accumulator of a systolic array vary significantly across different voltage-frequency operating points. At the software level, we propose an Accumulation-Aware Training approach that incorporates this information as a first-order optimization constraint during training. By integrating mixed-precision quantization with unstructured pruning, we effectively constrain the model's weight distribution to avoid activating the hardware's sensitized high-rank carry chains. At the architectural level, we complement this with selective MSB-masking and fine-grained clock-gating to minimize unnecessary dynamic switching activity. Furthermore, we employ a layer-wise Dynamic Frequency Scaling strategy to further adapt to the accumulator's reduced dynamic range, based on the numerical sensitivity of each layer. We experimentally evaluate the proposed solution on a deep scaled 2nm technology node and demonstrate that, when operating at a low-voltage frontier, our co-optimized post-layout architecture delivers substantial energy and energy-delay product gains of 39% and 23% respectively, relative to the nominal voltage implementation, while maintaining accuracy degradation within 1%.

Abstract
Video world models (VWMs) are rapidly emerging as scalable neural simulators for robotic manipulation. However, most benchmarks only evaluate them as open-world video generators, using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that lack strict 3D spatial and kinematic reasoning. To bridge this gap, we introduce RoboWorM, a comprehensive, deployment-aware benchmark that leverages known hardware priors such as robot kinematic trees and fixed camera configurations, to systematically examine structural and spatiotemporal fidelity. Using our metrics, we achieve 2.3$\times$ maximum inference speed-up with minimal quality loss.

Abstract
Deploying a language-guided navigation policy in a new building usually means watching it fail. Public-benchmark VLAs degrade scene by scene as layout, appearance, and language drift, and the standard fix of teleoperating fresh trajectories on site does not scale. Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated impressive performance when integrated into robot manipulation pipelines, but real-world evaluation of VLM capabilities on downstream robotics tasks is both difficult and expensive to scale. We propose that spatial and temporal ordering tasks using images from stereo and wrist cameras attached to real-world robots are particularly well-suited for evaluating visual reasoning capabilities, as they assess skills directly applicable to robots. Abstract
World-action models (WAM) predict future states to guide robot actions, enabling learning from both action-free video and action-labeled interaction. Most inherit pretrained video generators, leaving WAM pretraining and scaling underexplored. We introduce Genie Envisioner Act 2.0 (GE-Act 2.0), a world-action model whose trainable generative and action components are all initialized from scratch on manipulation data. It combines a control-oriented autoencoder (CoAE), a single-step visual planner (SVP), and an inverse dynamics model (IDM). CoAE retains action- and instruction-relevant information under aggressive compression, while SVP produces a complete future state in one differentiable pass, so visual planning and inverse dynamics can be pretrained separately on complementary data. The components are then jointly trained with knowledge-aligned selective optimization (KASO), which reduces mismatched supervision by selecting only predicted futures judged behaviorally compatible with the recorded action. Gains span 19/20 and 18/20 skill groups, and skill-specific coverage strongly correlates with zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) success (Pearson r=0.80; Spearman rho=0.85). Under the same protocol, the model grounds object, color, shape, and position references in at least 90% of trials and follows explicit instructions even when they conflict with an already-committed behavior or a conventional scene association.

Authors: Xiaodi Huang, Zilin Wang, Jinpeng Liu, Pengchuan Xiao, Zhenlei Shao, Zhang Zhang, Shuxiang Lu

Abstract
Embodied perception requires positional representations that capture both physical geometry and the spatial and temporal extent of observations. We introduce POD-RoPE (Physical Observation Distribution RoPE), which represents token observation footprints as distributions over camera centers, viewing directions, and sampling times. Under independent sampling, expected rotations encode relative relationships between distributions; when each distribution collapses to a single address, the construction recovers pointwise RoPE. The same rotation statistics support Q/K matching and coherence-dependent V/O transformations, allowing observation geometry to guide both information selection and content aggregation. Per-token pre-aggregation avoids increasing sequence length or processing pairs of observation nodes. We evaluate POD-RoPE across four robot policy architectures, simulation and real-world manipulation, and complementary reconstruction, depth-estimation, and novel-view-synthesis tasks. Integrating POD-RoPE into Fast-WAM improves mean success from 91.83% to 92.88% across all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, and from 32.5% to 52.5% on two real-robot tasks on Sharpa North. Controlled reconstruction ablations support the contributions of distribution encoding and V/O transformations. In H20 model-block benchmarks, POD-RoPE achieves median forward-plus-backward speedups of $1.91\times$ over P-RoPE and $5.34\times$ over RayRoPE, with a median latency overhead of 17.4% over Standard RoPE. These results support physical observation distributions as an effective and computationally efficient basis for geometry-aware attention.

RoboJev: An Open Vision-Language Judge for Robot Success Detection

Abstract
Many robot manipulation videos do not indicate whether the task was successfully completed. RoboJev-27B is an open-weight vision-language model that takes a task instruction, 10 frames of a manipulation attempt, and (if available) final frames from auxiliary cameras and predicts whether the robot accomplished the task. RoboJev-27B is a variant of JEV-27B-VL, trained with a rank-32 LoRA adapter on the language model only (keeping the vision encoder frozen) using 52,598 episode records. It also uses a source-specific KL penalty that applies only to research data, anchoring predictions closer to the original model on those sources. It uses a calibrated two-token readout that outputs success/failure probabilities in a single forward pass. On 268 held-out episodes of the same robot model (SO-101), RoboJev-27B attains 95.2% balanced accuracy and 0.993 AUROC. It also generalises to episodes collected by unseen labs/robots (AUROC = 0.890 over 459 episodes) as well as to clearly labelled community-collected episodes (accuracy = 93.3% over 135 episodes), where the base model attains an AUROC = 0.900 on the unseen-lab/robot subset with the same inputs. It takes 0.26 seconds per episode (median, serial) to infer on a single H100, which is about \$0.19 per 1,000 success predictions at \$2.69 per GPU-hour (not including media processing and serving costs). RoboJev-27B is released together with its inference code, along with a transparent evaluation: 20,628 predictions organized into 29 model/protocol entries are made available to facilitate browsing and to enable reproducible benchmarking of success detection.
