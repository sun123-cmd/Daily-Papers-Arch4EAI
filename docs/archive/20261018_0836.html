LIBERO-PeRM: Benchmarking Personalized Robotic Manipulation
Authors: Zhixu Li, Keqian Tang, Litian Gong, Jingyu Yao, Wenqian Zhang, Tianze Xu, Zehao Wang, Yuping Wang, Junge Zhang, Jiachen Li
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-15T17:59:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:59:59+00:00
Comments: Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026); Project Website: https://libero-perm.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Personalization is essential for general-purpose robots to transition into household environments and achieve true practicality. While general robotic manipulation has been extensively studied, personalized robotic manipulation (PeRM) lacks a controlled benchmark for separating task completion from preference satisfaction. We introduce LIBERO-PeRM, a benchmark for preference learning in embodied agents. LIBERO-PeRM studies five questions: 1) how policies follow preferences in zero-shot settings; 2) how they learn single preferences from demonstrations; 3) how efficiently they acquire preferences as demonstrations increase; 4) how learned preferences generalize across layouts and tasks; and 5) how policies compose preferences and behave under conflicts. We propose a controlled formulation and taxonomy of observable preferences in simulation, covering motion, skill, and user-profile choices. This formulation is restricted to language-expressible preferences that can be instantiated in LIBERO without changing the task goal, evaluated automatically, and reused across tasks. Building on LIBERO, our procedural pipeline generates paired demonstrations with and without preferences. We create five task suites (312 tasks) with high-quality demonstrations, rich metadata, and preference-satisfaction metrics. Experiments show that current VLA policies struggle with zero-shot personalization, while SFT makes preferences learnable. Mixed training achieves better no-preference retention than sequential adaptation and improves performance with contextual prompts compared with preference-only training, but can weaken motion-preference learning; preference generalization, decomposition, and conflict handling remain difficult. The code, datasets, and demos can be found on the project website: https://libero-perm.github.io/.

EmbodiedMidtrain: Bridging the Gap between Vision-Language Models and Vision-Language-Action Models via Mid-training
Authors: Yiyang Du, Zhanqiu Guo, Haochen Zhang, Annabella Macaluso, Xin Ye, Liu Ren, Yonatan Bisk, Chenyan Xiong
First: 2026-04-21T21:40:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:55:44+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) inherit their visual and linguistic capabilities from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), yet most VLAs are built from off-the-shelf VLMs that are not adapted to the embodied domain, limiting their downstream performance. In this work, we propose EmbodiedMidtrain to bridge the gap between VLMs and VLAs. We first characterize the data distribution gap between them, showing that VLA data occupy compact regions that are largely separated from the broader VLM distribution, while the degree of alignment varies substantially both across and within VLM data sources. Then, we build a mid-training data engine that leverages a lightweight learnable proximity estimator to select the most VLA-aligned candidates from a large VLM pool, and produce EmbodiedMidtrain-1M, a VLM dataset that we further use to mid-train the VLM before downstream VLA fine-tuning. Experiments on both simulated robot manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks show consistent VLA performance gains over off-the-shelf checkpoints across VLM backbones and VLA action heads. These gains extend to downstream tasks not used to guide data selection. Further analysis reveals that mid-training provides a stronger initialization for VLA fine-tuning, with gains emerging from the earliest steps and widening throughout training. Moreover, the data engine captures both dataset-level and sample-level alignment signals, favoring spatial reasoning over text-centric tasks while preserving the diversity of the VLM data. We will release all code, data and models for future research.

Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning for Path-Safe Target Reaching in Robotic Harvesting of Hydroponic Lettuce
Authors: Al Bashir, Partho Ghose, Yibin Wang, Robert G. Hardin, Joshua Peeples, Genhua Niu, Azlan Zahid
First: 2026-10-15T17:54:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:54:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robotic harvesting requires precise manipulation to ensure accurate positioning of the end-effector, especially in a constraint workspace. This study introduces the Path-Safe Approach Proximal Policy Optimization (PSA-PPO), a target-conditioned local reaching framework designed for robotic harvesting of nutrient film technique (NFT)-hydroponic lettuce in greenhouses. The proposed PSA-PPO preserves the standard clipped PPO objective while introducing a harvesting-oriented policy formulation that integrates target-conditioned observations, corridor-aware spatial constraints, trajectory-history-based safety encoding, and task-specific success criteria for constrained robotic manipulation. PSAPPO was trained entirely in Isaac Lab and successfully transferred to a physical UR10e robotic arm without any real-world policy fine-tuning. In a controlled physical benchmark against 150 scheduled targets, PSA-PPO achieved the highest reaching rate of 94.7%, outperforming Cartesian+TOTG (86.0%), RRTConnect (88.0%), and RRT* (80.0%), while reducing median RMS jerk by 39.8 to 73.0% relative to the classical planners during the final approach segment. A formulation ablation evaluated over 200 simulation targets demonstrates that path-safety and side-corridor shaping are the principal drivers of harvesting-compatible performance: removing either term substantially degrades strict success even when terminal endpoint accuracy is preserved or improved, confirming that optimization toward the endpoint alone is an insufficient objective for constrained crop-proximal manipulation. Finally, PSA-PPO was integrated with an RGB-D vision module and an end-effector and evaluated as a complete greenhouse harvesting system across 144 lettuce harvesting attempts, achieving 94.4% vision-guided target reaching and 86.1% complete harvesting success.

Mem4Act: Learning Effective Latent Memory for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation
Authors: Yun Zhang, Zhiyu Huang, Tianhui Cai, Chen Tang, Wei Wang, Jiaqi Ma
First: 2026-10-15T17:51:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:51:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Long-horizon robotic manipulation fundamentally requires memory: agents must retain task-relevant information from past observations and interactions and use it to guide future actions. Existing approaches typically preserve raw historical observations, compressed visual tokens, or language summaries, but these representations either expose the action policy to increasingly long histories or risk discarding task-relevant temporal, semantic, and perceptual information. We introduce Mem4Act, a framework for learning effective latent memory as a fixed-size representation explicitly optimized for long-horizon robotic control. Mem4Act addresses three coupled challenges: how to efficiently cover long histories, what information memory should retain, and how to ensure the policy actually relies on memory. To this end, it organizes history via multi-granularity temporal aggregation, preserves complementary semantic and perceptual information through question-answering supervision and key-frame DINO feature reconstruction, and employs memory-first two-stage training to encourage greater use of memory by the action policy. The resulting memory captures both high-level task state and fine-grained perceptual evidence while providing a bounded interface to the action policy. Across RoboMME, RMBench, and four real-world manipulation tasks, Mem4Act achieves state-of-the-art performance on memory-dependent control, with success rates of 49.88%, 61.0%, and 81.5%, respectively. Extensive ablations further demonstrate the importance of temporal aggregation, complementary memory supervision, and memory-first training.

Abstract
Robotic manipulation policies have made rapid progress in recent years, yet most existing approaches give limited consideration to memory capabilities. Consequently, they struggle to solve tasks that require reasoning over historical observations and maintaining task-relevant information over time, which are common requirements in real-world manipulation scenarios. Although several memory-aware policies have been proposed, systematic evaluation of memory-dependent manipulation remains underexplored, and the relationship between architectural design choices and memory performance is still not well understood. To address this gap, we introduce RMBench, a simulation benchmark comprising 9 manipulation tasks that span multiple levels of memory complexity, enabling systematic evaluation of policy memory capabilities. Robust Multi-Agent Pursuit with Preemptive Communication via Reachability-Based Control Barrier Functions
Authors: Madison Book, Justin Bradley
First: 2026-10-15T16:50:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:50:41+00:00
Comments: 6 pages, 4 figures. Accepted to the 2026 IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Safe multi-agent coordination typically assumes what real-world, deployed, resource-constrained robots cannot provide: continuous, perfect state sharing. Robust control strategies must be complemented with robust communication strategies to deliver resilient deployments. In this work, we propose a unified control/communication strategy that provides safe control to guarantee inter-agent safety under intermittent communication and demonstrate its effectiveness in a pursuit-evasion scenario. Each agent computes the reachable set of neighboring agents based on the last state information received, then solves for a safe control input based on control barrier functions to ensure agents avoid collision. We prove that inter-agent collision avoidance is guaranteed provided communication staleness remains bounded. As the reachable set inflates between state transmissions, each agent reasons about when to preemptively transmit its state information to neighboring agents based on novel metrics characterizing uncertainty risk and kinematic risk. We implement and simulate a variety of pursuit-evasion scenarios and show that preemptive communication reduces messages by 77.5% compared to periodic and event-triggered baselines with zero collisions.

FAME: An FPGA-Based Platform for Approximate Multipliers Evaluation with Pattern-Guided DNN Retraining
Authors: Rappy Saha, Nima Amirafshar, Jude Haris, Nima Taherinejad, José Cano
First: 2026-09-15T18:39:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:46:31+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the 38th IEEE/SBC International Symposium on Computer Architecture and High Performance Computing (SBAC-PAD) 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Approximate multipliers can reduce hardware area and energy consumption in Deep Neural Network (DNN) inference; however, they introduce computational errors. Assessing the accuracy of numerous approximate multiplier designs across diverse DNN models and large-scale datasets remains challenging due to prohibitive evaluation times. This overhead primarily stems from the slow emulation of approximate multiplier behavior using look-up tables (LUTs) on CPU and GPU platforms. Moreover, the resulting accuracy degradation must be carefully quantified and, if necessary, mitigated (e.g., through retraining), further increasing the overall evaluation cost. To address these challenges, we propose FAME, an FPGA-based platform for evaluating approximate multipliers. The platform exploits the reconfigurable logic of Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs) to implement approximate multipliers directly in hardware, eliminating the need for LUT-based emulation on CPU/GPU platforms and thereby enabling efficient DNN inference while significantly reducing evaluation time on large datasets. Furthermore, we introduce a pattern-guided DNN retraining technique to mitigate accuracy degradation induced by approximate multipliers. Specifically, retraining is guided by multiplier-specific patterns to effectively recover potential accuracy losses. We evaluate FAME using two DNN models, ResNet-18 and MobileNetV2, on the ImageNet dataset across 27 approximate multipliers. During inference, our approach achieves up to a 3.47x speedup in approximate multiplier evaluation compared to prior LUT-based emulation methods. Furthermore, the proposed retraining technique improves accuracy by up to 65.5% over existing retraining approaches for the evaluated multipliers. The code is publicly available at: https://github.com/gicLAB/FAME

MOSS: Mode-Oriented Source Shaping for Robot Flow Matching Policies
Authors: Zirun Ma, Jindou Jia, Tuo An, Jiaqi Bai, Jiahua Dong, Jianfei Yang
First: 2026-10-15T16:44:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:44:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In robotic manipulation, the same observation often admits multiple feasible actions. Generative policies such as diffusion and flow matching naturally capture such multimodal behaviors, yet which mode is executed is left to random sampling and cannot be actively controlled. We propose Mode-Oriented Source Shaping (MOSS), which turns mode selection into an explicit choice of where generation starts. We first reveal, both empirically and theoretically, that deterministic flow transport induces mode-dependent structure in the source space. Under ideal deterministic ODE transport, distinct behavioral modes have distinct source-space preimages, while practical approximation errors can blur their boundaries due to imbalanced data, limited model capacity, and few inference steps. Motivated by this source-space structure, MOSS constructs semantically aligned mode-specific Gaussian sources that encode mode identity in the initial noise. A modality-strength estimator predicts the local degree of multimodality, which adaptively controls the geometry of the mode-specific sources. It contracts them toward a shared Gaussian where behaviors are similar and separates them near behavioral branching points. During deployment, the desired behavior is selected at inference simply by sampling from the corresponding component. Across 6 simulated and 3 real-world manipulation tasks, MOSS steers to the target mode up to 100% of the time with high task success, recovers minority modes at over 90% guidance rate under a 10:90 imbalance dataset, and surpasses mode-conditioned flow policy by 43.3 points of guidance rate on real robots.

In robotic manipulation, the same observation often admits multiple feasible actions.

Abstract
We introduce REKI, a 2.4B vision-language-action model (VLA) pretrained exclusively on public data that sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size: on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365, it is outperformed only by models about twice its size. REKI consists of a vision-language model (VLM) followed by a flow-matching action expert, and is pretrained on a curated mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision-language data. Additionally, we endow it with a memory that gives the policy access to its recent past at negligible cost: a few register tokens in the action expert summarise the output of the VLM at each frame and persist in the expert's cache. This memory improves success in simulation and on real robots, and makes oscillations between poses about four times rarer. REKI uses a simple model design and training paradigm, and is optimised to be efficient at training and inference. We release the full code across data curation, training, inference and evaluation, deployment, and all pretrained and finetuned model weights: https://github.com/kyutai-labs/reki.

Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models present a promising paradigm for general-purpose robot manipulation, but closed-loop deployment is often limited by inference efficiency: at each timestep, high-resolution, often multi-view images are encoded into hundreds of visual tokens that dominate transformer computation and latency. Token compression via attention is a natural solution but is challenging for VLA control because (1) decision-critical tokens are determined by the task, not generic visual saliency, and (2) the token budget varies substantially across steps, so static metrics and budgets waste computation or discard control-critical tokens. We introduce FastVLA, a training-free, task-aware, and temporally adaptive visual token compression framework. At each step, FastVLA scores token importance via task-to-vision attention and adaptively sets the compression rate from the importance-distribution uniformity, measured by an inverse-Simpson metric with online sliding-window calibration. We evaluate FastVLA on OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and CogACT across LIBERO and SIMPLER, and on a physical Franka Research 3 robot. In simulation, it reduces LLM-backbone FLOPs by 35-59\% and action latency by 9-32\% (a 1.09$\times$-1.46$\times$ speedup) while maintaining task success, achieving a comparable or better efficiency-performance trade-off than existing plug-and-play token compression/pruning/reuse baselines. On the real robot, it cuts end-to-end latency by 20\% (1.25$\times$ speedup) at comparable success. Code is available at https://github.com/jiuyixu25/FastVLA.

Authors: Xueli Sun, Yuxiang Lu, Yantong Liu, Xianzhe Fan, Xi Chen, Zhe Liu, Hengshuang Zhao

Abstract
Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are inherently stochastic: different noise seeds can produce action chunks of varying quality from the same observation. At test time, however, the policy typically commits to a single sample without an explicit estimate of its quality. In fine-grained manipulation, executing a suboptimal action can irreversibly disrupt task progress and lead to failure. To exploit policy stochasticity, we introduce a training-free two-axis trigger that uses two initial samples to measure within-chunk temporal variation and cross-sample variance, detecting the critical states. Once triggered, a JEPA-based latent world model ranks candidate action chunks by predicting action-conditioned future latent states and the corresponding changes in task progress. By operating in latent space, the verifier avoids pixel reconstruction and enables efficient and more accurate ranking. Across three real-world tasks, $\textit{i.e.,}$ pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement), our method raises the average completion score from 50.8% to 65.5%, while adding 326 ms for sampling and verification per triggered decision on a single RTX 4090. Consistent gains are also observed in simulation, where the average success rate rises from 66.4% to 73.6% in the clean setting of RoboTwin 2.0, from 62.8% to 69.6% in its randomized setting, and from 96.9% to 97.6% on LIBERO.

What Should Robot Policies Learn to Predict? Rethinking Supervision Targets in Vision-Language-Action Learning
Authors: Haitao Shen, Hanyu Liu, Yihan Lin, Huiping Liu, Junbo Cui, Daoheng Li, Yang Li, Jing Zhang, Wenxin Li, Yuan Yao
First: 2026-10-15T14:01:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T14:01:06+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, 8 figures. Haitao Shen and Hanyu Liu contributed equally
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are typically trained with action prediction as the primary supervision signal, since robots ultimately execute actions. Auxiliary objectives such as latent-action (LA) and future-vision (FV) prediction are often added to improve policy learning, yet they compete with action prediction for the same training budget. We ask whether, under a fixed budget, allocating a substantial share of training samples to such objectives, rather than to further action prediction, can improve policy learning. We build a unified VLA framework that couples a two-stream multimodal diffusion transformer (MMDiT) with a sample-level router, which assigns each training sample to either action prediction or an auxiliary objective and thus makes supervision allocation explicit. Within this framework, we instantiate three LA targets based on LAPA and its DINO-feature variants, and three FV targets based on DINOv3, V-JEPA 2, and Wan2.2 representations. Across LIBERO, CALVIN, and four real-robot tasks, suitable LA and FV supervision outperforms action-only training under the same budget, even when action prediction receives no more than half of the training samples. FV generally outperforms LA, although the strongest LA target remains competitive on CALVIN. Diagnostic analyses suggest that DINO-based LA targets emphasize relative motion, whereas FV targets emphasize absolute position and task identity. We therefore argue that what robot policies learn to predict, and how training is allocated among these targets, should be treated as explicit design choices rather than defaulting to action-dominant training.

Abstract
Continuous physical human-robot interaction requires robots to rapidly adapt their control behaviour without interrupting task execution. However, existing ROS 2 control architectures rely on stop-to-switch mechanisms that introduce substantial delays and interrupt execution. We present an open-source, sub-millisecond reflex engine based on an enhanced ROS 2-compatible multimode controller that enables deterministic switching between control modalities and integrates reflex behaviours for mitigating contact hazards and kinematic limit violations. Experiments on a Franka Robotics manipulator demonstrate switching delays below 0.1 ms, more than four orders of magnitude faster than conventional ros2_control, while maintaining continuous operation during safety-critical events. In a multi-stage manipulation task, the proposed framework reduced execution time by 3.5% and peak trajectory jerk by more than threefold. By eliminating runtime middleware overhead, controller transitions are implemented as lock-free pointer operations, enabling reflex-level responsiveness within an open-source robotics framework.

Compiling Multi-Threshold Operators for Quantized Non-Monotonic and Fine-Granular Activations in Custom-Tailored DNN Accelerators
Authors: Christoph Berganski, Felix Jentzsch, Marco Platzner
First: 2026-10-15T13:09:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T13:09:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Aggressive quantization of neural networks can expose activation functions as bottlenecks for efficient hardware acceleration. Implementing quantized activations as multi-threshold operations has been shown to be a promising optimization, allowing for efficient hardware implementation of few-bit activations. However, current implementations are limited to monotonically increasing functions, quantization schemes with one-dimensional (per-channel) granularity and a small set of predefined activation-quantizer combinations. We formalize the multi-threshold representation to extend it to non-monotonic functions and fine-granular parameters, i.e., multi-dimensional thresholds. For automated conversion of quantized function compositions into threshold representation, we propose a systematic approach via algebraic rules integrated into the ONNX-based compiler infrastructure. We experimentally validate our solution on a selection of monotonic and non-monotonic functions by extending the binary search based multi-threshold operator that is integrated into the FINN framework for end-to-end synthesis of custom-tailored FPGA accelerators. The cost of hardware support for non-monotonicity and fine-granularity is characterized in comparison to an alternative approach utilizing existing operators via graph-level decomposition. We compare our method to prior work as well as the approach taken by the hls4ml framework. As an outlook, we demonstrate the prospects of using the multi-threshold representation during the training of a neural network as a form of quantization aware training.

Cross-Embodiment Causal Transfer from Human Videos to Robot Manipulation
Authors: Xingyu Zhang, Yingchao Li, Peizheng Guo, Jie Hu, Changwen Zheng, Wenwen Qiang

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Scaling robot manipulation models requires large amounts of interaction data, yet collecting diverse real-robot experience remains costly. Human manipulation videos, in contrast, provide abundant and diverse task experience that could potentially benefit robot learning. However, differences in morphology, action spaces, and control mechanisms make human motions unsuitable as direct supervision for robot actions, raising a scientific question: what knowledge in human videos should be transferred across embodiments to robot manipulation? To address this question, we analyze subtask transfer through the lens of causal beams, focusing on which contextual conditions and interaction dependencies should be retained to support local task effects. Based on this perspective, we propose Causal-Beam-Guided Subtask Transfer (CBST). CBST learns manipulation-related representations from human videos and grounds them in task-level semantic variables using robot demonstrations. Within a predefined manipulation model, causal-beam constraints guide contextual information selection by requiring retained assignments to sustain local mechanism outputs while preserving nontrivial responsiveness to retained inputs. The selected semantic information conditions a world-action model, allowing the robot to generate actions from its own observations without requiring human-robot motion correspondence. Experiments on robot manipulation benchmarks show improvements in task success over the corresponding world-action model baselines.

Don't Regenerate, Debug: A Domain-Specific Agent for Repairing Near-Miss Accelerator Kernels
Authors: Yansong Sun, Shenxiu Wu, Siyuan Chen, Runlin Hou, Junhao Qiu, Junming Cao, Shudi Shao, Zhichao Lu, Qingfu Zhang
First: 2026-08-03T17:59:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T12:33:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Kernel generation for hardware accelerators such as GPUs and NPUs has become a proving ground for large language models (LLMs), and state-of-the-art systems raise correctness through pipelines that couple LLMs with agentic reinforcement learning and evolutionary search. Such pipelines generate, compile, and execute large numbers of candidate kernels, discarding most of them and forgoing the opportunity to distill failures into reusable knowledge. Many discarded candidates are near-miss operators that compile and run but fail numerical validation; each embodies genuine domain knowledge and a nontrivial investment in LLM inference, cross-compilation, and hardware execution. We argue for a paradigm shift: rather than regenerate, debug. Debugging is far more constrained than generating from scratch: the search space is small and feedback is dense. We present a domain-specific debug agent that addresses three core challenges in autonomous repair: mitigating knowledge scarcity through retrieved patterns and diagnostic instrumentation, ensuring integrity through anti-cheat detection and full-coverage evaluation, and controlling cost via convergence guards and bounded iteration. Debugging serves two complementary roles: it extends the capability frontier by recovering operators that repeated regeneration fails to produce, and it lowers cost per deliverable operator. Debug Pass@1 achieves 66.7% versus Regenerate Avg Pass@1's 25.9% and Regenerate Pass@3's 40.7%, while consuming 92.8% fewer tokens per success than three-trial regeneration. Component ablations show that the knowledge base drives recovery, while integrity gates reject 12.5-33.3% of the successes the workflow itself accepted.

Abstract
Echo State Networks (ESNs), as lightweight implementations of reservoir computing, provide efficient solutions for temporal data processing in resource-constrained environments. While standard ESNs offer computational efficiency, their dense readout layers inflate the stored model and the per-sample inference cost. This paper introduces a Smooth-L1 regularized ESN (SL1ESN) that promotes sparsity in the readout layer while preserving differentiability for gradient-based optimization. The method employs a twice-differentiable Smooth-L1 approximation compatible with Newton's method, which we exploit both for centralized training and for a second-order (Newton-step) federated aggregation scheme. The resulting sparsity compresses the stored model, the per-sample inference cost, and the server-to-client broadcast by a comparable factor. These results demonstrate the practical value of sparse readouts for edge computing and IoT deployments.
